    Quarterbacks

    Tier 1

    1. Josh Allen, Buffalo Bills

    Allen has finished among the top three quarterbacks in fantasy points per game in each of the past six seasons, making him the most consistent fantasy asset at the position over that span. He has thrown for at least 3,600 yards and 25 touchdowns in every one of those seasons while rushing for at least 400 yards every year of his career and scoring 12 or more rushing touchdowns in each of the past three.

    Those rushing scores are not just coming from short-yardage situations. Allen continues to add chunk plays as a runner, including a 40-yard touchdown this past season, and what sets him apart historically is the balance between his two skill sets. He is a more polished passer than most elite rushing quarterbacks and a more dangerous runner than most high-end pocket passers.

    The one real concern is age. Allen turns 30 before the season starts, and while quarterbacks tend to age well as passers, elite rushing production typically declines earlier. Michael Vick and Cam Newton both posted 500-plus-yard rushing seasons at 31, and Russell Wilson and Rich Gannon reached that mark at 32 or older, but those were outliers within otherwise declining rushing profiles. Allen is entering relatively uncharted territory as a high-volume fantasy rusher at this stage of his career.

    There is also a path to a bigger 2026 season. Allen posted a career-high in yards per attempt last year but a career-low in pass attempts per game, resulting in his lowest passing yards per game since 2019. Buffalo leaned more heavily on the run in part because of a thin receiving corps, but the Bills addressed that by trading for DJ Moore as their new top outside receiver and drafting Skyler Bell in the fourth round as a slot option. Those additions should push Allen's passing numbers back up in 2026.

    In redraft, he is the top quarterback option by a clear margin. In dynasty, it is reasonable to weigh younger quarterbacks for long-term roster construction, but it is hard to pass on a player who is overwhelmingly likely to finish as a top-two fantasy quarterback for the next several seasons.

    Tier 2

    2. Drake Maye, New England Patriots

    Maye delivered one of the best sophomore seasons by a quarterback in the past two decades. The only other quarterbacks to earn a PFF grade of 90.0 or higher in their second season were Patrick Mahomes, Lamar Jackson, Joe Burrow and Justin Herbert, and among that group Maye posted the third-highest passing grade and the third-highest rushing grade.

    That performance translated directly into fantasy production, with Maye ranking second among quarterbacks in total fantasy points and third in points per game. The Patriots retained most of their offense and added A.J. Brown and Romeo Doubs at wide receiver, giving Maye a significantly stronger supporting cast than he had a year ago. For a player of his age and résumé, there is a strong case to make him the top overall asset in dynasty formats.

    That said, there are reasons to account for some regression. Quarterbacks who consistently finish inside the top three in fantasy points tend to produce elite rushing numbers, and six of the nine top-three fantasy finishes from 2022 through 2024 came from quarterbacks who rushed for at least 500 yards. Maye totaled 409 in 2025, and it is possible that one or two quarterbacks with heavier rushing workloads provide a higher weekly ceiling. His passing efficiency metrics also point toward at least modest normalization, as his 8.9 yards per attempt led the league while his 6.3% touchdown rate and 1.6% interception rate are both difficult to sustain.

    One common argument against Maye centered on schedule strength in 2025, with several of his best games coming against favorable defenses. However, he averaged 21.9 fantasy points per game across five matchups against top-eight defenses in fewest fantasy points allowed, with only one poor outing in that stretch against Buffalo, which is a meaningful counterpoint to the schedule concerns.

    3. Jalen Hurts, Philadelphia Eagles

    Hurts has finished among the top eight quarterbacks in fantasy points per game in each of the past five seasons, a mark only Josh Allen can match over that span. Lamar Jackson has four such seasons and Patrick Mahomes has three, making Hurts the second-most consistent fantasy quarterback in recent years behind Allen, and the youngest of the group.

    All four of those quarterbacks had down seasons in 2025, and there are real reasons to think the others face more headwinds going forward. Mahomes is recovering from a torn ACL and LCL with Week 1 availability uncertain, Jackson has an injury history and a supporting cast that is aging, and the concerns around Rashee Rice and Travis Kelce in Kansas City are well documented. That context makes Hurts one of the safer options at the position heading into 2026.

    That said, 2025 was below his usual standard. His 7.1 yards per attempt was a career low, and his 26.3 rushing yards per game represented a sharp drop from the prior season and roughly half of his career high. He also converted fewer rushing touchdowns, offsetting them with more passing scores, which is positive for Philadelphia's offense but less impactful in four-point passing touchdown formats.

    The Eagles ranked outside the top 10 in PFF offensive team grade for the first time since 2020 and finished below league average in points scored. Philadelphia traded A.J. Brown and replaced him with Makai Lemon, Dontayvion Wicks, Hollywood Brown and tight end Eli Stowers, and even without Brown the Eagles should have a top-10 offense given the overall talent on the roster. New offensive coordinator Sean Mannion has also indicated that Hurts' rushing ability will remain a central part of the scheme, which should help his fantasy value bounce back toward his typical range.

    4. Lamar Jackson, Baltimore Ravens

    Jackson is the highest-risk, highest-reward quarterback in the top five. He is the only quarterback to ever average 25 fantasy points per game in multiple seasons — having done so in both 2019 and 2024 — and he has won the NFL MVP twice while nearly claiming a third in 2024.

    The concern is his declining rushing volume. Jackson averaged double-digit carries per game from 2019 through 2021 before falling to 9.3 in both 2022 and 2023, 8.2 in 2024 and just 5.2 in 2025. Some rebound is reasonable to expect, but a return to his peak rushing workload is not.

    His passing efficiency has remained strong, as he has averaged more than 8.0 yards per attempt in each of the past three seasons. However, his sack rate climbed to a career high in 2025, contributing to a decline in passing volume compared to the previous two seasons.

    The Ravens also overhauled their coaching staff, hiring Jesse Minter as head coach and Declan Doyle as offensive coordinator. Doyle spent 2025 with the Chicago Bears but did not call plays under head coach Ben Johnson, making 2026 the first season he will run his own offense. At 29, he is one of the youngest and least experienced play-callers to inherit an elite quarterback, and some growing pains are to be expected.

    Baltimore also lost several notable offensive players in free agency, including center Tyler Linderbaum, tight ends Isaiah Likely and Charlie Kolar, wide receiver DeAndre Hopkins and fullback Patrick Ricard. The offense now leans on Derrick Henry, Mark Andrews and Ronnie Stanley, all of whom are on the wrong side of 30. Baltimore also used seven draft picks on offensive players, and how quickly those rookies contribute will have a meaningful impact on Jackson's floor and ceiling in 2026. Even with his league-winning upside, the uncertainty surrounding his rushing workload, new coaching staff and supporting cast makes his top-five price difficult to justify when similar upside is available several rounds later.

    5. Jayden Daniels, Washington Commanders

    Daniels was a top-five fantasy quarterback as a rookie, averaging 21.5 fantasy points per game on the strength of his rushing, with 148 carries for 891 yards and six touchdowns. No quarterback was close to those marks, with Josh Allen‘s 112 carries for 579 yards coming in second for the position.

    However, Daniels struggled significantly in 2025, largely due to injury. He dealt with a knee sprain, hamstring strain and dislocated elbow across the season, with his only fully healthy game coming in Week 1 — when he ran 11 times and scored 20.1 fantasy points.

    His rushing ability gives him the potential to be the top fantasy quarterback over the next several seasons if he can stay healthy, including a realistic shot at the top spot in 2026. The elite rushing quarterbacks of the past decade have all gradually run less often, and they remain near the top of these rankings largely because no one has stepped in to replace them.

    The biggest concern is that Daniels continues to incur injuries, or that his injury history leads him to become more conservative as a runner. There is some comfort in the fact that he does not need to match his rookie rushing volume to remain a top fantasy quarterback, but any meaningful reduction in that area would significantly lower his potential.

    The other worry is significant turnover in Daniels’ supporting cast. David Blough takes over as offensive coordinator after spending two seasons as the team's assistant quarterbacks coach, providing the offense with some continuity with Daniels while also marking his first time calling plays at the NFL level. Five of the six Commanders with 40 or more receptions over the past two seasons are off the roster, including Zach Ertz, Deebo Samuel, Olamide Zaccheaus, Austin Ekeler and Noah Brown. Terry McLaurin is the only significant returning receiver, with third-round rookie Antonio Williams, tight end Chig Okonkwo and running back Rachaad White among the notable additions. Whether those changes help or hurt the offense immediately adds another layer of uncertainty around Daniels heading into 2026.

    6. Joe Burrow, Cincinnati Bengals

    Burrow opened the 2025 season slowly, drawing the Cleveland Browns in Week 1 and then suffering an injury in Week 2. From his return in Week 13 through Week 18, he ranked third among quarterbacks in fantasy points per game. He finished as the QB3 in 2024.

    Unlike most quarterbacks drafted near the top of fantasy leagues, Burrow does not rely on rushing production. He is the exception because he has been one of the highest-graded passers in the NFL in recent seasons, operates in one of the league's most pass-heavy offenses and throws to elite wide receivers in Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins.

    Continuity also works in his favor, as Cincinnati is retaining its coaching staff and Burrow, Chase, and Higgins are all signed long-term. Twenty of the 22 players who logged at least 25 offensive snaps last season remain on the roster. The only departures are backup tight end Noah Fant and third-string quarterback Jake Browning, with Erick All Jr. returning from injury and veteran Josh Johnson filling those roles.

    Health remains the primary concern. Burrow has missed at least six games in three of his six NFL seasons and has already won Comeback Player of the Year twice. His injuries have affected different areas of his body, including a knee, wrist and toe, which suggests misfortune rather than a recurring structural issue.

    The secondary concern is whether an improved defense reduces his passing volume. The Bengals have ranked among the worst defenses in terms of points allowed in each of the past two seasons, forcing them into negative game scripts and a pass-heavy approach. Cincinnati addressed that by trading for defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence and signing safety Bryan Cook and defensive end Boye Mafe in free agency. If the defense improves significantly, the Bengals should spend more time protecting fourth-quarter leads with the running game, which would likely reduce Burrow's passing volume and cap his fantasy ceiling. His floor is as safe as any quarterback's, but potential volume regression and his injury history make it difficult to justify his cost when similar production is available several rounds later.

    Tier 3

    7. Justin Herbert, Los Angeles Chargers

    Herbert was the rare rookie quarterback in 2020 who finished among the top 10 at the position despite not being known for his rushing. He placed second in 2021 and 10th in 2022, then missed time in 2023 but still ranked top 12 in fantasy points per game. Los Angeles leaned more run-heavy under offensive coordinator Greg Roman, yet Herbert still finished 16th and 10th over the past two seasons.

    Expectations are higher this season under new offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel. Los Angeles has a young core of skill players around him: running back Omarion Hampton, receivers Ladd McConkey, Quentin Johnston and Tre Harris and tight end Oronde Gadsden II. The offensive line should also be much improved, with Rashawn Slater and Joe Alt healthy and three new interior linemen.

    Typically, quarterbacks run more in their first season or two in the league and gradually take off less as their careers progress. Herbert was relatively consistent over his first five seasons but then ran six times per game last season while averaging 5.3 yards per carry, good for 31.1 rushing yards per game. His 498 rushing yards were the second-most among quarterbacks.

    The stars are aligned for Herbert to be one of the top fantasy quarterbacks this season, if not the top. If all goes well and he turns a few of those carries into rushing touchdowns, he could finish in the top tier. But this wouldn't be the first season he entered with overall QB1 potential only for injuries to his supporting cast to derail it.

    In single-quarterback leagues, I am comfortable passing on every quarterback drafted in the first few rounds and waiting on Herbert. It is still worth adding a second quality option, though, particularly for weeks when Herbert draws a rough matchup.

    8. Trevor Lawrence, Jacksonville Jaguars

    Lawrence spent four years as the Jaguars' starter to mixed results, with some stretches where he looked like a Pro Bowl-caliber quarterback and others where he played like a backup. In 2022 and 2023, his fantasy production made him a high-end backup. But in 2024, his passing yards per game fell by more than 45 amid lower volume, and then an injury cut his season short.

    He became a top sleeper quarterback in 2025 after the Jaguars hired Liam Coen as head coach and drafted Travis Hunter with the second overall pick to pair with Brian Thomas Jr., who mostly excelled after Lawrence's injury. Lawrence finished fourth in fantasy points and sixth in points per game at 20.6, though that owed more to his connection with Parker Washington, Jakobi Meyers and Brenton Strange than to Thomas and Hunter. Lawrence also set career highs in rushing attempts, rushing yards and rushing touchdowns.

    Some regression in Lawrence’s nine rushing touchdowns is reasonable to expect, but his overall outlook should be similar to last season. Lawrence will have all five of his receivers available at once, allowing for a rotation and no single player for a defense to key on.

    That should make Lawrence a top-10 option again, yet he is being drafted outside the top 10 since there are several quarterbacks to feel good about this season. That makes him a strong value at his current ADP.

    9. Brock Purdy, San Francisco 49ers

    Purdy consistently outperforms his ADP in fantasy points per game. He has ranked among the top 12 quarterbacks in each of his three seasons as a starter, and that includes stretches with elite receiving options like Brandon Aiyuk, Deebo Samuel, Jauan Jennings, George Kittle and Christian McCaffrey all on the field together, as well as stretches when most of his intended skill players were hurt. Last season, his wide receivers were Jennings, Ricky Pearsall, Demarcus Robinson and Kendrick Bourne.

    He is currently being drafted outside the top 12 quarterbacks but is worth one of those picks based on his play. Purdy has a 91.1 offensive grade over the past three seasons, sixth among quarterbacks, behind Josh Allen, Joe Burrow, Lamar Jackson, Matthew Stafford and Justin Herbert.

    San Francisco invested in its receiver room this offseason, adding veterans Mike Evans and Christian Kirk and drafting De'Zhaun Stribling in the second round. They join Pearsall and should give the 49ers their best receiver group in years.

    The biggest concern for Purdy is how much he leans on veterans near the end of their careers. Christian McCaffrey, Kyle Juszczyk, Mike Evans, George Kittle and Trent Williams are all among the oldest starters at their positions. There is a decent chance one or more of them starts to decline, dragging down the whole offense, in which case San Francisco will need its younger players to step up.

    Purdy is one of a handful of undervalued quarterbacks, which makes waiting at the position an easier decision.

    10. Caleb Williams, Chicago Bears

    Williams was a non-factor in single-quarterback leagues as a rookie, but the additions of Ben Johnson at head coach, Luther Burden III at wide receiver and Colston Loveland at tight end helped propel him to 19.0 fantasy points per game last season, tied for eighth-most among quarterbacks.

    Williams was not a consistent passer on a play-by-play basis. His 57.0% accuracy was second-worst among the 28 quarterbacks with at least 300 attempts, and his 23.6% uncatchable rate was the worst. Despite that, he managed a 71.1 regular-season PFF passing grade, right in the middle of the pack. His fantasy production was buoyed by volume and a strong 27-to-9 touchdown-to-interception ratio.

    Williams also ran 77 times for 383 yards, tied for seventh-most among quarterbacks, giving him a nice floor. Some statistical regression is likely given his accuracy concerns, but there is also reason to expect improvement in his second year in Ben Johnson's system. Williams’ young receivers played exceptionally well down the stretch last season, including in the playoffs, and should build on that momentum.

    Williams should be considered a fantasy starter this season. Given his age and likely long-term future with Johnson, Burden and Loveland, he ranks among the top dynasty options at quarterback.

    11. Dak Prescott, Dallas Cowboys

    Prescott has spent a decade as the Cowboys' starting quarterback, with varying levels of fantasy success. He ranked eighth in fantasy points per game among quarterbacks last season, but was 22nd in 2024. He ranked among the top five in fantasy points per game in both 2019 and 2020, the latter cut to five games by a season-ending ankle injury, but that was in the first half of his career.

    His PFF grades have fluctuated over time, with passing grades ranging from 67 to 87 and little consistency from one season to the next. He ranks seventh among quarterbacks in passing grade over the last three seasons combined. Prescott offers minimal rushing production. He ran 53 times last season, but 20 of those were kneel-downs or fumbles that statistically went down as rushing attempts.

    On the bright side, almost everyone related to the offense remains the same as last season. Prescott has two of the best wide receivers at his disposal, a strong offensive line and a coaching staff willing to throw the ball.

    Tier 4

    12. Kyler Murray, Minnesota Vikings

    Murray finished as a top-12 fantasy quarterback in points per game in each of the first five seasons of his career. His fantasy value declined in 2024 because of injury, as his rushing attempts per game dropped to 4.6. That number rebounded to 5.8 in 2025, but his fantasy production did not follow, as he posted career lows in yards per attempt and passing yards per game across five healthy starts.

    Murray joins a Vikings offense led by Kevin O'Connell, whose system has consistently produced fantasy-relevant quarterbacks outside of last season. Minnesota typically operates at an average-to-high pace and consistently ranks among the league leaders in pass rate. Sam Darnold finished as a top-10 fantasy quarterback in 2024, while Kirk Cousins did so in 2022 and ranked in the top 10 in fantasy points per game in 2023. After Cousins suffered a season-ending injury in 2023, Joshua Dobbs concluded as a top-12 fantasy quarterback in three of the four games in which he played every offensive snap, while Nick Mullens placed among the top 14 in games where he handled the full workload. Both quarterbacks also posted top-five weekly finishes.

    The Vikings' offense struggled last season, largely because of inconsistent quarterback play and persistent injuries along the offensive line. Center Ryan Kelly missed half the season, while Christian Darrisaw and Brian O'Neill were also sidelined for significant time and played through injuries when available. The addition of Murray, combined with improved health up front, should help the Vikings’ offense return to its typical level of play.

    It is also worth noting that Murray performed at his best alongside an elite wide receiver. While he played with several strong starters in Arizona, his best stretch came with DeAndre Hopkins from 2020 to 2022. Hopkins' final All-Pro season came in 2020, the same year Murray finished as the overall QB2. Murray now joins forces with Justin Jefferson, who has earned higher PFF receiving grades in recent seasons than Hopkins did during his time with the Cardinals.

    If both Murray and the Vikings’ offense return to their expected level of play, he has a chance to be a consistent fantasy starter in single-quarterback leagues.

    13. Bo Nix, Denver Broncos

    Nix has spent the past two seasons as the Broncos' starting quarterback. He has finished seventh in fantasy points each year, though he has benefited from staying healthy both regular seasons, and he has ranked ninth and 11th in points per game.

    His 75.9 PFF passing grade over the past two seasons ranks 20th among the 45 quarterbacks with at least 45 dropbacks, so he has outproduced that mark for fantasy purposes for a few reasons. He has leaned heavily on volume, ranking second in total dropbacks at 1,402. Denver has had a top-10 defense, and Nix has had the best pass-blocking offensive line in each of the past two seasons. That line has stayed remarkably healthy, with its most important members, Garett Bolles and Quinn Meinerz, not missing a game and the other three each playing at least 1,500 snaps over the span.

    The good news is that running is part of Nix's game. He ranks fifth in rushing attempts and eighth in rushing yards over the past two seasons. He can't elude tacklers the way some rushing quarterbacks can, but his willingness to run has helped make him a top-10 fantasy quarterback both years. Denver also traded for Jaylen Waddle this offseason, giving him a new top weapon, which should help his passing efficiency.

    The downside is that Denver could lean on the run this season. J.K. Dobbins is healthy again, the team should trust RJ Harvey more in his second year, and it drafted Jonah Coleman. A team with a great defense typically pairs it with heavy rushing once it has the lead, which could cut into Nix's volume. He may also run less himself, having fractured his right ankle in the divisional round last year, and while all signs point to him playing in Week 1, he might not run as often. Some regression in the offensive line's health is possible too.

    It is entirely possible Nix looks like a better quarterback this year while posting worse fantasy production. His ADP is rightfully a bit lower than where he has finished the past two years. He is one of several quarterbacks who should be paired with another in single-quarterback leagues.

    14. Jaxson Dart, New York Giants

    Dart was highly impressive as a rookie from a fantasy perspective, averaging 20.5 fantasy points per start, seventh among quarterbacks. His rushing ability drove much of that production, as his 86 rushing attempts ranked fourth at the position and resulted in nine rushing touchdowns. As a passer, however, he averaged just 6.7 yards per attempt and threw 15 touchdown passes.

    There are several reasons to expect improvement from Dart and the Giants' passing game this season. By the end of last year, his top receiving options were Darius Slayton, Isaiah Hodgins, Gunner Olszewski and tight end Daniel Bellinger. This season, Malik Nabers is expected to be healthy, while the Giants added Darnell Mooney, Calvin Austin III and Malachi Fields at wide receiver and Isaiah Likely at tight end. Running back Cam Skattebo and tight end Theo Johnson should also be healthy to begin the season, and New York used the 10th overall pick on offensive lineman Francis Mauigoa to further bolster the offense.

    The biggest question is Nabers' health. If he is not ready for Week 1 or is limited during the season, Dart's chances of joining the top tier of fantasy quarterbacks become much slimmer.

    Injury concerns are the primary reason Dart is not ranked higher. He missed two games because of a concussion and underwent concussion evaluations several other times. While he could adjust his playing style to reduce the number of head impacts, additional concussions would increase the risk of missed time. Ranking him outside the top tier reflects that risk.

    15. Patrick Mahomes, Kansas City Chiefs

    At his best, Mahomes is the league's best quarterback. He averaged 26.1 fantasy points per game in 2018 despite only 272 rushing yards and two rushing touchdowns. He finished first again in 2022 and was a clear-cut fantasy starter in every year between. He fell outside the top 10 fantasy quarterbacks in 2023 and 2024 before jumping back to a tie for second last season.

    Part of that decline came with a drop in passing grade. He posted three seasons at 89.0 or better in his first five years as a starter, then slid steadily to 70.7 last season. His average target depth fell from 9.5 at his peak to 6.8 and 6.9 in 2023 and 2024. It climbed back to 8.3 last season, which helped his yards per attempt and yards per game, but also brought more negatively graded and less accurate throws.

    The big reason for his fantasy bounce-back was his rushing. He ran once more per game than the previous season, averaged 1.3 more yards per carry and scored three more touchdowns. That added 2.5 fantasy points per game, while his passing rose by just 0.3.

    Mahomes then suffered a torn ACL and LCL. Signs point to him playing in Week 1, but he is unlikely to keep running as much as he did last season because of the injury. There are also question marks around his receivers, as Travis Kelce is another year older, Rashee Rice could face a suspension at some point, and Xavier Worthy had a down year.

    The good news is that Eric Bieniemy is back as offensive coordinator. He held the role from 2018 to 2022, when Mahomes was at his fantasy best. If Kansas City returns to the offense that made him a fantasy star and MVP, that could lift his production despite the concerns.

    In single-quarterback leagues, Mahomes should be drafted alongside another option, so whoever has the best matchup that week can slot into your lineup.

    16. Matthew Stafford, Los Angeles Rams

    Stafford was the league's MVP last season. He led the league in passing yards per game at 276.9, only slightly above his career average. His 7.9 yards per attempt tied for the third-best mark of his career. What really set him apart statistically was his touchdowns and interceptions. He threw a touchdown on 7.7% of his passes, a career high, and an interception on just 1.3%, a career low outside of his 2010 season, when he played only three games.

    All of that added up to the fourth-most fantasy points per game at 21.1, but significant regression is fair to expect. If his touchdown rate falls back to his 2023 rate, his best season from 2022 to 2024, he would have averaged 4.3 fewer fantasy points per game, which would have put him at QB18. He coincidentally finished as QB18 that season.

    Stafford's age, 38, is also a concern. His play clearly didn't regress last season, but at some point a decline is inevitable. There is also a chance Los Angeles is simply too good this season. The Rams led the league in points scored but ranked only 10th in points allowed. They added Myles Garrett, Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson to the defense, and there are at least rumors that Aaron Donald could join them. If that defense improves as expected, the Rams won't need to throw as much and will spend the fourth quarter running the ball with backups.

    Stafford might only belong in fantasy starting lineups in weeks the game projects to be close, which means he shouldn't be drafted as a starter in single-quarterback leagues. He is also a notable case of a quarterback ranked much lower in dynasty than in redraft, given his age.

    17. Jared Goff, Detroit Lions

    Goff has spent the last five seasons as the Lions' starting quarterback and hasn't missed a game in the last four seasons. He's finished the last four seasons in the QB6-QB10 range in total points, but has finished outside the top 12 quarterbacks in fantasy points per game in three of those four seasons.

    His 85.0 passing grade over the last four seasons is 10th-best among quarterbacks. His passing production has been elevated by his coaching staff, skill players and offensive line. Goff has thrown for 18,205 yards over the last four years, the best mark among quarterbacks by over 1,000 yards. He's accomplished this despite Patrick Mahomes and Justin Herbert having more dropbacks.

    Goff was one of three quarterbacks last season with over 4,000 passing yards but fewer than 100 rushing. Most of Goff's official rushing attempts were either kneel downs or fumbles. His only designed runs for positive gains were three sneaks, and he scrambled five times. The lack of rushing production puts a major damper on his high-end fantasy value. The last quarterback to average over 22 fantasy points per game with fewer than 200 rushing yards was Tom Brady in 2021, and he posted the third-most passing attempts in a season in NFL history to reach enough fantasy volume. Brady only finished at QB3 that season.

    Quarterbacks like Matthew Stafford, Goff and Sam Darnold, who have great receivers in high-scoring offenses, aren't being drafted as starters in single-quarterback leagues, because it's more likely their passing volume decreases than increases, and there isn't much reason to expect notable increases in rushing production. Quarterbacks with more rushing upside are generally favored in other high-scoring offenses. However, Goff is one of several quarterbacks who are great to pick up late, pair with another solid fantasy option, and start whichever quarterback has the best matchup that week.

    18. Baker Mayfield, Tampa Bay Buccaneers

    Mayfield’s 2025 did not go as expected. His touchdown rate returned to his career norm, and his 6.8 yards per attempt was the second-lowest of his career. Further, Mayfield’s accuracy rate fell from 61.3% to 55.9%, while his uncatchable pass rate climbed from 16.0% to 23.1%, and his accuracy on deep and intermediate passes dropped by 12%.

    Mayfield dealt with several injuries, including an oblique and a shoulder issue, which likely drove the accuracy dip. He also played through leg injuries but kept running at a strong rate, similar to 2024. That jump came from a higher rushing success rate that lifted his yards per game, along with a big spike in touchdowns.

    Mayfield’s career rate of passing attempts becoming touchdowns is 5.0%, but in 2024, it was 7.2%. He also became much more productive as a rusher, averaging 22.2 yards per game after 9.6 the previous year.

    That jump came from a higher success rate that lifted his yards per game, along with a large growth in touchdowns. His career rate of passing attempts becoming touchdowns is 5.0%, but in 2024 it was 7.2%. He also became much more productive as a rusher, averaging 22.2 yards per game after 9.6 the previous year.

    Expectations for Mayfield should land somewhere between his 2023 and 2024 seasons. His touchdown rate is unlikely to peak like 2024 again, but his rushing floor should stay higher, as it has the past two years. Tampa Bay lost Mike Evans but still has a strong receiver group in Emeka Egbuka, Chris Godwin and Jalen McMillan. The Buccaneers have a new offensive coordinator in Zac Robinson, though the offense should resemble the strong 2024 version under Liam Coen. Robinson and Coen worked together for several years in Los Angeles, including 2022, when Mayfield was there too.

    Mayfield isn't ranked among the top 12 quarterbacks given the uncertainty of a new coordinator, the loss of Evans and how he plays coming back from several injuries. But, he is a strong later-round option who can start when the matchup is right.

    19. Jordan Love, Green Bay Packers

    Love has spent the past three seasons as the Packers' starter, ranking seventh (19.4), 16th (16.3) and 21st (16.1) in fantasy points per game. The big reason for the decline has simply been volume. He averaged 34 attempts per game in 2023, and that fell to 28.3 in 2024 and 29.3 in 2025. A small part of that is the games he left with injuries the past two years, but he threw at least 30 times in 13 games in 2023, more than he managed across the two seasons since combined.

    Love has been among the highest-graded quarterbacks, his 86.6 passing grade ranking fifth over the past two seasons, but the Packers have had no problem leaning on the run. They rank 31st in dropbacks and fourth in rushing attempts. There is a chance Green Bay throws more this season. Tucker Kraft emerged as one of the league's top receiving tight ends last season before an injury, Christian Watson and Jayden Reed both showed enough promise to earn extensions this offseason, and Matthew Golden is expected to have a larger role in his second year.

    The one big concern is the declining offensive line. Green Bay ranked third in pass-block grade in 2024, with Elgton Jenkins, Zach Tom and Rasheed Walker all grading in the 80s, but the team fell to 21st last season. Jenkins and Walker both declined while remaining among its top three linemen in pass protection, and neither is on the roster now. Sean Rhyan and Aaron Banks remain starters despite never posting a PFF pass-blocking grade of 70.0 or better. Jordan Morgan is moving to left tackle full time, which adds uncertainty, and Anthony Belton struggled as a rookie and will need to improve. That uncertainty will make Love's life harder, but it could also mean less effective rushing, which would push the team to throw more.

    Love is a top-10 quarterback, and with a different offensive philosophy or better players around him, he would be a top-10 fantasy quarterback. Without that, he is a safe QB2, one who can start weekly in superflex leagues and in the right matchups elsewhere. If some of Green Bay's young skill players improve, Love could become a weekly starter in single-quarterback leagues.

    20. Malik Willis, Miami Dolphins

    Willis spent two seasons as a backup for the Tennessee Titans but received only three starts. Tennessee drafted Will Levis 33rd overall a year after taking Willis 86th, which made the latter expendable. The Packers traded for Willis to serve as a backup, and over two years, he made three starts and saw notable playing time in three other games.

    Willis has dropped back to pass just 118 times over the past two years, but his 79.9 PFF passing grade ranks 15th among all quarterbacks. Most important for fantasy, he scrambled on 15.1% of his dropbacks over that span, which leads all quarterbacks. That was notably ahead of Jayden Daniels, Tyrod Taylor, Justin Fields, Drake Maye and Jaxson Dart.

    Willis signed with the Miami Dolphins to be their starting quarterback this season. Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley and general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan were both with the Packers before joining Miami. Some passing regression is fair to expect, since Willis won't have the same weapons in Miami that he had in Green Bay. Defenses will also be better prepared for him, since his appearances in Green Bay were often a surprise; because he plays so differently from Jordan Love, defenses weren't ready for him.

    Even if Willis’ scrambles per dropback regress somewhat, he should remain among the league leaders in quarterback rushing production, which is a big plus for his fantasy value. Extrapolating his rushing attempts per start and yards per carry in Green Bay, he would have posted 119 rushing attempts and 738 rushing yards over a season, both of which would have led all quarterbacks last year. It's fair not to expect numbers quite that big in Miami, but he could be a fantasy starter if he holds the job and scores enough touchdowns.

    Willis is a strong option for anyone willing to wait before adding a second quarterback in redraft leagues.

    21. Tyler Shough, New Orleans Saints

    Shough was drafted 40th overall by the Saints in 2025 and took over as the team's starter partway through Week 8. From Weeks 9 to 18, he averaged 17.6 PPR points per game, 12th among quarterbacks. He had the right combination of volume as both a passer and runner, along with good efficiency for a rookie.

    He benefited from an easier-than-average schedule. He faced a top-five defense against fantasy quarterbacks three times and posted two top-10 finishes in those games. In three more against teams in the top half of the league at limiting quarterback fantasy points, he had his other top-10 finish. His remaining three starts came against bottom-half teams, and he failed to crack the top 10 in all three.

    The Saints leaned heavily on Shough last season. Alvin Kamara and Kendre Miller were both hurt for most of his time as a starter, and the team had traded away Rashid Shaheed and cut Brandin Cooks, leaving it thin at two important positions. New Orleans signed Travis Etienne and drafted Jordyn Tyson, which should help Shough's passing efficiency but could also push the Saints to run more.

    Shough's ADP is relatively low because there's usually a reason to prefer other quarterbacks, whether more experience at a high level, a better supporting cast or more rushing upside. The likeliest outcome is he regresses somewhat despite the upgraded weapons, but there's a chance Etienne and Tyson lift the offense to a new level and Shough improves on his 17.6 PPR points per game.

    He is a clear example of how deep the quarterback position is this year, and a reason it makes sense to wait at quarterback in single-quarterback leagues. Shough can be drafted in the later rounds and slotted into lineups when the matchup is right.

    22. Sam Darnold, Seattle Seahawks

    Darnold is a Super Bowl-winning quarterback with an 83.8 passing grade over the past two seasons, eighth among quarterbacks. Despite the offense playing well, he ranked 27th in fantasy points per game last season at 14.7. The problem was volume, as the Seahawks ranked second in designed rushing plays and second-to-last in dropbacks.

    He topped 34 attempts in four games, including two of their three losses, an overtime win and a two-point win, but was held to 27 or fewer in 10 of 17 games. His attempts fell as low as 12 in a 44-22 win over the Arizona Cardinals in which he played more than 90% of the offensive snaps.

    There is reason to believe Darnold could throw more this season. Seattle did not re-sign Kenneth Walker III, drafting Jadarian Price instead, and Zach Charbonnet is expected to miss the start of the season. The Seahawks also re-signed Rashid Shaheed, who will now have a full offseason working with Darnold. If the run game struggles, the passing game should be able to pick up the slack.

    Seattle also had the league's best scoring defense last season, which is likely to regress at least somewhat, since defensive production tends to regress more than offensive. The team lost safety Coby Bryant, cornerback Tariq Woolen and edge rusher Boye Mafe, replacing them with rookies and free agents mostly expected to be backups. A step back on defense would also push the offense to do more, which again means more passing.

    Darnold is a fine late-round option with real reason to expect better fantasy production, though the question is how much. He ran for only 95 yards last season, so there is a clear ceiling on his rushing. Even if he doesn't finish among the top 12 fantasy quarterbacks, he can be started in weeks that project as close games rather than clear Seattle wins.

    23. C.J. Stroud, Houston Texans

    Stroud has spent three seasons as the Houston Texans' starting quarterback. His PFF passing grade started out excellent as a rookie, at 79.6, but has regressed every year since, down to 70.0 last season. His fantasy finishes have followed the same path, 10th as a rookie and outside the top 20 in each of the last two seasons.

    A quarterback who grades like Stroud usually posts better fantasy results, but Houston's offensive line has held him back. He has the eighth-most dropbacks under pressure over the past three seasons. When kept clean over that span, he averaged 8.1 yards per attempt, tied with Drake Maye and Matthew Stafford for fifth among quarterbacks, but his 5.9 mark under pressure sits right at league average.

    The Texans largely reworked the line, signing three free agents and drafting Keylan Rutledge with their first-round pick. Rookie linemen tend to struggle, though, and the two free agents slated to start are on the wrong side of 30 and have already seen their PFF grades slip from their peak. There is a chance the line improves, but perhaps not by much.

    The good news is Houston's young receiver core. Nico Collins has been among the league's best for a few years, Jayden Higgins and Jaylin Noel both flashed as rookies, and Tank Dell should return this season. The other factor, good for the Texans but not for Stroud, is that Houston has one of the league's best defenses, which means it won't allow many points. The addition of David Montgomery should mean more rushing, and the team could lean conservative if that approach is working.

    That makes Stroud someone to mostly avoid in single-quarterback redraft leagues, though he remains an intriguing dynasty option. The receivers should stay with him for a few years, the offensive line should trend up rather than down, and the defense is likely to regress at some point. If Stroud improves as a passer on top of that, it is a perfect storm for him to become a much better fantasy option over the next few seasons.

    24. Daniel Jones, Indianapolis Colts

    Jones has spent seven years as an NFL starter, with wildly different fantasy results. He topped out in 2022 with the ninth-most fantasy points per game but has also finished outside the top 30 multiple times. Last season, his first with the Colts, he finished 12th at 18.0 fantasy points per game.

    Jones’ 73.2 career PFF passing grade ranks sixth-lowest among the 29 quarterbacks with at least 2,000 dropbacks, and three of the quarterbacks below him are now backups. His single-season grade has hovered between 65.0 and 71.0 most years, so there's little reason to expect a substantial jump in his passing quality.

    A big part of Jones’ earlier success was his rushing. In his best season, 2022, he ran 120 times for 708 yards and seven touchdowns. That production has steadily declined since, and last season, he posted career lows of 3.5 attempts and 12.6 yards per game, though he did score five rushing touchdowns.

    Those scores helped him to a strong year in Indianapolis, and he also posted a career-high 8.6 average depth of target. That raised Jones’ big-time throw rate but also his turnover-worthy play clip, a combination that generally helps fantasy production.

    Jones then suffered a ruptured Achilles that ended his season early. He is expected to be ready for Week 1, but he is unlikely to run for as many touchdowns. The Colts also traded away Michael Pittman Jr. and lost right tackle Braden Smith in free agency without making substantial additions, so the offense could take a small step back.

    Jones is a fine second option in superflex leagues but should be drafted in single-quarterback redraft leagues only if the benches are deeper than usual.

    Running Backs

    Tier 1

    1. Jahmyr Gibbs, Detroit Lions

    Jahmyr Gibbs is the only running back to average at least 21 PPR points per game in each of the last two seasons, doing so by averaging over 5.0 yards per carry, scoring double-digit rushing touchdowns, and being the only back with over 500 receiving yards over that span — all behind one of the best offensive lines in the league.

    This offseason, Detroit moved on from David Montgomery, whose presence had previously limited Gibbs' ceiling at the goal line. While that impact was less pronounced in 2025, Gibbs could still see an increase in rushing touchdowns with Montgomery gone. And while the Lions added Isiah Pacheco in free agency as a backup, he is unlikely to affect Gibbs' overall volume in a meaningful way.

    Detroit also hired former Cardinals offensive coordinator Drew Petzing to run the offense. Arizona occasionally deployed a two-back committee under Petzing, but he has also shown a willingness to heavily feature a lead back, as James Conner regularly posted snap shares above 75%. A major shift in Gibbs' usage is not expected.

    The competition for touches between Amon-Ra St. Brown, Jameson Williams, Sam LaPorta and Isaac TeSlaa is a slight negative, but Gibbs dealt with the same group last season and still averaged over 21 points per game. Those same receivers also help Detroit field one of the best offenses in the NFL, contributing to the touchdown opportunities that have defined his production.

    The biggest concern is the offensive line. Detroit ranked second in run-blocking grade in both 2023 and 2024 before dropping to 13th last season following the losses of Frank Ragnow and Kevin Zeitler. The Lions made two more changes this offseason, moving on from left tackle Taylor Decker and center Graham Glasgow and replacing them with first-round rookie Blake Miller and fifth-year center Cade Mays. These are roughly lateral moves on paper, but the continued turnover adds uncertainty to an area that has been central to Gibbs' success.

    The other concern is his contract situation, as he is currently in the midst of a “hold-in” with Detroit. In the vast majority of cases, though, a player who is holding out or holding in at the start of August is ready to play in Week 1. Fantasy managers shouldn't worry unless a month passes with Gibbs still not practicing and threatening to sit.

    Ultimately, some of these changes could prove to be steps forward rather than back, and the other elite running backs in this range carry even more uncertainty heading into 2026. This makes Gibbs the top option in redraft leagues regardless of format.

    2. Bijan Robinson, Atlanta Falcons

    Robinson has finished among the top-three fantasy running backs in each of the last two seasons, gaining over 1,400 rushing yards and catching at least 60 passes each year. No running back has been as consistent in both aspects of the game over that span.

    Atlanta reshaped its backfield this offseason, with Tyler Allgeier departing in free agency and Brian Robinson Jr. brought in as his replacement. Like Allgeier, Brian Robinson is a bigger back who could siphon some goal-line work from Bijan Robinson. However, Allgeier graded higher than Brian Robinson, making this transition a lateral move at worst and a slight positive for Bijan Robinson's overall outlook.

    The Falcons also hired Kevin Stefanski as head coach. Stefanski has often deployed a two-back rotation, though that tendency was influenced in part by Nick Chubb's limited receiving role in Cleveland. In 2019 as Minnesota's offensive coordinator, Stefanski featured Dalvin Cook heavily, with Cook playing over 70% of the team's offensive snaps when healthy and finishing as the PPR RB2. Stefanski has also shown a willingness to lean on one back when warranted, as Kareem Hunt logged games above an 85% snap share when Chubb was unavailable. His history of prioritizing the running back position should work in Robinson's favor.

    The primary concern is the offensive line. The unit has been a strength throughout Robinson's career, but 34-year-old left tackle Jake Matthews posted a career-low run-blocking grade last season. The team had expected Kaleb McGary to return at right tackle after missing last season due to injury, and his return was the top reason for optimism heading into the offseason. Instead, McGary opted to retire, leading Atlanta to sign Jawaan Taylor, who is similarly a stronger pass protector than run blocker.

    Robinson is ultimately competing with Jahmyr Gibbs for the top running back spot, but Gibbs holds an edge thanks to the Lions' offensive success as a whole and the concerns about Atlanta's offensive tackles.

    Tier 2

    3. Christian McCaffrey, San Francisco 49ers

    McCaffrey led all running backs in fantasy points last season, the fifth time he has finished among the top two at the position. He did so in an unusual way, ranking 10th among running backs in rushing fantasy production while delivering receiving production that would have ranked among the top 10 wide receivers.

    Despite ranking second in rushing attempts, McCaffrey averaged just 3.9 yards per carry and was less effective at generating explosive runs. NGS tracking data also indicates his top-end speed has declined over the past two seasons compared to earlier in his career. A rebound in rushing efficiency is possible, but there is also a scenario in which San Francisco begins shifting more carries to other backs.

    His receiving production was also influenced by a depleted 49ers pass-catching group. San Francisco expected to feature Brandon Aiyuk, Jauan Jennings, Ricky Pearsall and George Kittle, but injuries disrupted that plan all season. Aiyuk did not play, Pearsall missed six games and struggled upon return, and Kittle missed five games and was limited early after he came back. Jennings was constantly on the injury report.

    The 49ers addressed the issue by adding veteran wide receivers Mike Evans and Christian Kirk, bringing back Samuel, and drafting De'Zhaun Stribling 33rd overall. This combination should help form a much stronger receiving corps, which could reduce McCaffrey's target volume.

    All three of the top running backs rely on elite offensive line play, particularly from standout individuals: right guard Chris Lindstrom in Atlanta, right tackle Penei Sewell in Detroit and left tackle Trent Williams in San Francisco. Williams will be 38 next season, making him the most likely of the group to decline or miss time.

    McCaffrey still carries overall RB1 upside if everything breaks right, but more indicators point toward regression, given how his production was generated and the early signs of decline in his profile. That, combined with his age and an injury history that includes 13 missed games in 2020, nine in 2021 and 13 in 2024, moves him out of the top tier of running backs and makes him a mid-first-round pick in redraft.

    4. Jonathan Taylor, Indianapolis Colts

    For most of the 2025 season, Taylor provided more rewards than risks. He was the only running back with double-digit +1 graded runs to go with five or more runs graded +1.5 or better. Almost all of that came in the first 12 weeks, when he averaged 25.7 PPR points per game. He had only one run of +1 or better over the final third of the season, a stretch in which he averaged 13.3 PPR points per game. That boom-or-bust style, a few big runs when things are going well but mixed results otherwise, has left Taylor with a 71.4 PFF grade over the past two seasons, lower than those of the backs usually drafted in the first two rounds.

    Despite that relatively low grade and inconsistent play, Taylor remains a top-five fantasy option after the Colts once again declined to make a meaningful investment behind him. Indianapolis added D.J. Giddens in the fifth round and Khalil Herbert in free agency ahead of the 2025 season, but neither emerged as a reliable complementary option. The Colts ultimately turned to veteran Ameer Abdullah as the primary backup.

    Taylor went on to play 84.2% of Indianapolis's offensive snaps in 2025, the highest rate among NFL running backs. The Colts then let Abdullah depart this offseason, replacing him only with seventh-round rookie Seth McGowan and a group of undrafted free agents. That approach strongly suggests Indianapolis remains comfortable handing Taylor one of the league's heaviest workloads.

    He does come with injury concerns. He played every game last season but missed at least three games in each of the three seasons before that. There is also some uncertainty at quarterback, with Daniel Jones recovering from injury. On the bright side, Taylor keeps his head coach and offensive coordinator.

    He has the potential to be the top overall back if he and Jones stay healthy and both play as well as they did over the first half of last season, and he carries a decent floor as long as he's healthy. He's worthy of a first-round pick, but a good example of how even first-round picks aren't sure things.

    Tier 3

    5. De'Von Achane, Miami Dolphins

    Achane has ranked among the top six running backs in fantasy points per game in each of the past three seasons. He also led all running backs in PFF rushing grade (91.0) last season, but his situation has not been as favorable as that of the top three backs.

    The Falcons, Lions and 49ers have all ranked among the top 12 in PFF run-blocking grade in each of the past four seasons, while the Dolphins have finished in the bottom five in each of the past two. Miami has also ranked in the bottom 12 in scoring in both seasons, making it more difficult for Achane to match the touchdown production of his peers.

    The Dolphins moved on from head coach Mike McDaniel, as well as key players Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill and Jaylen Waddle this offseason. Their departures might lead to a slight uptick in touches for Achane, but he already ranked seventh in the league at 19.0 last season. There might be room for one or two additional touches per game, but his volume is already historic for someone of his size.

    There is a concern that the offense could take a step back. That could mean fewer rushing attempts and more receptions, which could help his PPR value, but it could also lead to fewer touchdown opportunities.

    The fact that he is among the most talented backs in the league, along with being the obvious focal point of the offense, should keep Achane as a safe top-10 running back, but there is enough holding him back to make it unlikely he finishes among the top few players.

    6. James Cook III, Buffalo Bills

    Cook finished 11th among running backs in fantasy points per game in 2024, driven in large part by 16 rushing touchdowns. That level of scoring led to expectations of regression, which pushed his draft cost down. Several factors from this past season, though, suggest he can remain a high-level producer in 2026.

    His workload increased significantly, with carries per game rising from 12.9 to 19.1. That jump was driven partly by Buffalo's more run-heavy approach and partly by a reduced role for Ray Davis. Cook also maintained strong efficiency, averaging 5.3 yards per carry, slightly above his 5.1 career mark. Just as important, the added rushing volume did not come at the expense of his receiving usage, which held steady with the previous season.

    Buffalo's offensive line also took a step forward, ranking sixth in PFF run-blocking grade. Right tackle Spencer Brown led the unit with the best season of his career, and the rest of the line provided steady play. Cook's PFF rushing grade dipped slightly, but the improved blocking helped lift his overall rushing efficiency.

    The Bills' offseason changes should have minimal impact on his outlook. The running back depth chart is unchanged. The team lost starting guard David Edwards, but his expected replacement, Alec Anderson, has graded well as a run blocker in limited action. Buffalo also added wide receiver DJ Moore, who should help the offense overall without necessarily pushing it toward a more pass-heavy approach.

    Cook is well positioned to deliver similar fantasy value in 2026. The main concern is a slightly reduced workload. He has stayed remarkably healthy for a running back over his first four seasons, especially as one of the smaller backs in the NFL at 5-foot-11 and 190 pounds. Buffalo could opt to give him fewer touches in games they are winning, keeping him fresh for the stretch run.

    7. Ashton Jeanty, Las Vegas Raiders

    Jeanty was selected sixth overall in the 2025 NFL Draft, a draft slot that typically supports immediate top-10 fantasy production. However, several factors outside of his control limited his rookie output.

    The Raiders scored just 241 offensive points last season, the fewest in the NFL, and ranked third-lowest in PFF run-blocking grade (53.0). As a result, Jeanty averaged just 0.6 yards before contact per attempt, the second-lowest mark among running backs with at least 100 carries.

    Despite those conditions, Jeanty forced 61 missed tackles on the ground, ranking fifth among running backs. He also ranked fourth in PFF rushing grade (92.3) on carries with positive yards before contact, highlighting his ability to capitalize when given space and suggesting significant upside in an improved environment.

    Las Vegas has a new head coach this season, and all indications point to the team having selected quarterback Fernando Mendoza with the first overall pick. The Raiders also entered the offseason with significant cap space and invested in the offensive line by adding center Tyler Linderbaum, which should help improve Jeanty's efficiency.

    The primary remaining concern is the backfield behind him. The Raiders added fourth-round rookie Mike Washington Jr., a new big backup. Kubiak, who came over from the Seattle Seahawks, consistently deployed a two-back approach there, with Zach Charbonnet (220 pounds) regularly stealing goal-line touches from Kenneth Walker III (211). Now he has a back heavier than Charbonnet in Washington (223) and a back lighter than Walker in Jeanty (208). While Jeanty still has an opportunity to be among the league leaders in touches, a lack of goal-line work will hurt his chances of finishing among the top-5 fantasy running backs this season.

    Tier 4

    8. Chase Brown, Cincinnati Bengals

    Brown ranked 10th among running backs in fantasy points per game in 2024 and seventh in 2025, and there is little reason to expect a significant change in 2026.

    He benefits from playing in a high-scoring offense where defenses are primarily focused on containing Joe Burrow, Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. Brown ranked seventh among running backs in carries against light boxes while seeing far fewer opportunities against stacked fronts, making his job consistently easier on the ground.

    He is also heavily involved in the passing game, ranking third among running backs in first-read targets over the past two seasons and second in checkdown targets. When Burrow extends plays, Brown is among the most frequently targeted options, tied with Samaje Perine.

    Cincinnati made no significant changes to its backfield in free agency, retaining Perine, Tahj Brooks, Gary Brightwell and Kendall Milton. The most notable offensive change was the departure of rotational tight end Noah Fant, though Erick All Jr. is expected to return from injury and fill that role again.

    The key variable for Brown is Burrow's health. When Burrow returned from injury in Weeks 13 through 18 last season, Brown averaged 22.3 PPR points per game, second-best among running backs in that stretch, and did so without breaking a run longer than 25 yards. If both stay healthy, Brown has a realistic path to finishing as a top-five fantasy running back.

    9. Kenneth Walker, Kansas City Chiefs

    Walker has been one of the NFL's most efficient running backs on a per-play basis over the past three seasons. He is tied for first among running backs in PFF rushing grade (93.7) over that span, matching De'Von Achane, while his 77.3 PFF receiving grade ranks 11th among running backs with at least 1,000 snaps. Overall, he ranks second in PFF overall grade (93.2), trailing only Derrick Henry.

    That efficiency has not fully translated to fantasy success, particularly this past season. Walker split backfield work with Zach Charbonnet, who ranks 12th among running backs in PFF overall grade over the same three-year span. Charbonnet handled a significant share of goal-line work, limiting Walker's touchdown production, while Seattle's offense reduced running back involvement in the passing game, cutting into both players' reception totals.

    Walker now joins a Chiefs backfield that moved on from Isiah Pacheco and has not re-signed Kareem Hunt. Kansas City added third-down specialist Emari Demercado in free agency and selected Emmett Johnson in the fifth round, giving the rookie a chance to compete with Brashard Smith for rotational snaps.

    The Chiefs were the NFL's second-most pass-heavy offense last season, throwing on roughly two-thirds of their offensive plays, and have ranked among the league's most pass-oriented teams in five of the past six seasons, consistently favoring the pass near the goal line as well.

    At minimum, Walker projects as a weekly fantasy starter. He has shown promise as a receiver in limited opportunities, and Kansas City has historically targeted running backs at a higher rate than most offenses. While Demercado or Johnson could siphon some third-down snaps, Walker's experience in two-minute situations may help him carve out a larger overall role. If his usage expands in the passing game or Andy Reid leans more heavily on the run than in recent seasons, Walker has the upside to finish as a top-five fantasy running back.

    10. Omarion Hampton, Los Angeles Chargers

    Hampton posted one excellent game in his rookie season, running 12 times for 128 yards and a touchdown while catching five passes for 37 yards against the New York Giants. He averaged less than 4.0 yards per carry in four of his other five games before an ankle fracture cost him half the season. He returned for the final four games of the regular season but struggled more often than not, then played just two offensive snaps in the Chargers' playoff loss.

    Like many rookies, Hampton wasn't as efficient as fantasy managers had hoped, in part because of the line blocking for him. He averaged a below-average 1.1 yards before contact per attempt, but his 3.4 yards after contact per attempt ranked 10th among running backs.

    The Chargers' offensive line looks completely different this year. Rashawn Slater missed all of 2025 but was a top-five run-blocking tackle in 2024, and Joe Alt was the team's highest-graded run blocker last season despite missing most of the year with injury. They also added center Tyler Biadasz, whose 71.2 run-block grade towers over the 51.1 mark from former center Bradley Bozeman. A similar gap exists between new right guard Cole Strange and his predecessor, Mekhi Becton. There will be a competition at left guard, but that spot should ideally be an upgrade as well.

    The major concern is his competition for touches. Kimani Vidal played well in Hampton's absence and was notably the better pass protector. Los Angeles also added Keaton Mitchell in free agency, the running back most similar to De'Von Achane. New offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel, who helped Achane become a top-five fantasy back in Miami, heavily campaigned for general manager Joe Hortiz to add Mitchell.

    If McDaniel elevates the Chargers‘ offense into one of the league's best and Hampton handles the clear majority of backfield touches, he could be in for a special season. But plenty could go wrong, from how the carries are divided to the several key players coming off injuries who may not return to form.

    11. Saquon Barkley, Philadelphia Eagles

    Barkley averaged 22.2 fantasy points per game in 2024 as fantasy's top running back. He ran for 2,005 yards and 13 touchdowns. There were concerns when he joined Philadelphia around his touchdown total with Jalen Hurts' tush-push and around his receiving production. Neither of those mattered in 2024, because he was breaking long runs for touchdowns at a historic rate.

    Those long breakout runs regressed as expected, and he averaged only 14.5 PPR points per game, ranking 15th-best among running backs. All five offensive line spots took a step back in run-block grade. Injuries were a major concern for the line throughout the season. While Lane Johnson was the only player to miss significant time, all three interior linemen played through injuries during the season.

    It's unlikely Barkley will return to being the top overall running back, but there are plenty of reasons for optimism. The Eagles retained the entire offensive line, who will hopefully be healthy this season. The top three running backs on the Eagles' depth chart should remain the same from last season, and most of the offensive players remain the same, outside of losing A.J. Brown. New offensive coordinator Sean Mannion should help take advantage of the talent Philadelphia has on offense. There is a chance Barkley is targeted more than in the last two seasons in Mannion's offense.

    The biggest concern is that Barkley might not see as many touches. Tank Bigsby received an 89.1 rushing grade last season compared to 76.7 by Barkley. He had the same offensive line issues, but averaged 4.0 yards after contact per attempt, compared to 2.7 by Barkley. Barkley is 29, which is on the older side for running backs, while Bigsby will turn 25. Barkley averaged 19.8 touches per game last season, ranking sixth-best among running backs, but the Eagles might decide it's okay to sacrifice a few touches per game to keep Barkley fresher when they have a suitable replacement behind him. Even with more efficiency this season, fewer touches could bring him into RB2 range.

    12. Kyren Williams, Los Angeles Rams

    There is a lot to like about Williams going forward, but his backup, Blake Corum, is one significant problem.

    Williams is one of four running backs to finish top 12 in fantasy points per game in each of the last three seasons, though all three of the others rank ahead of him. His 80.8 PFF offensive grade places 16th at the position.

    He benefits from stability in one of the league's best offenses. The Rams' 93.3 PFF offensive team grade was the best in the NFL this past season, and they retained 18 of their 19 offensive players with at least 100 offensive snaps, losing only Rob Havenstein to retirement. Los Angeles also made significant investments on defense, acquiring Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie via trade and adding Jaylen Watson in free agency. That improved defense should help the Rams build bigger leads and run the ball more frequently. Sean McVay also returns as head coach, though the Rams did lose offensive coordinator Mike LaFleur to the Arizona Cardinals.

    The one real concern is that Williams spent the second half of last season in a drive-by-drive rotation with Corum. The two posted identical 87.0 PFF rushing grades during the 2025 regular season, with Williams showing more consistency on a per-play basis while Corum was the better big-play threat.

    A similar rotation seems likely in 2026, which limits Williams' chances of finishing among the top-five running backs. That said, if Corum suffers an injury, or either back takes a meaningful step in at least one direction, Williams has top-five potential.

    His edge as the more accomplished receiving back means he should hold the most fantasy value, even if the rotation continues. Williams is very safe as a weekly fantasy starter given his talent and the quality of the Rams‘ offense.

    13. Derrick Henry, Baltimore Ravens

    Henry has spent nearly a decade as a must-start fantasy option whenever he's healthy. He averaged 18.1 rushing attempts and 93.9 rushing yards per game last season, scoring 16 rushing touchdowns for the second straight season. He finished eighth among running backs in both fantasy points and fantasy points per game.

    The big drawback with Henry has been his receiving production. He caught only 15 passes last season, while every other running back in the top 20 in fantasy points caught at least 30. That is unlikely to change and will continue to limit his chances of finishing as one of the top few backs in the league.

    There is reason to expect at least some regression from Henry this season. The Ravens lost center Tyler Linderbaum, fullback Patrick Ricard and tight end Charlie Kolar in free agency, and they ranked fourth, second and 10th at their positions, respectively, in PFF run-blocking grade last season. He averaged 1.6 yards before contact per carry, ranking seventh among running backs. Part of that was the threat of Lamar Jackson as a runner, but part of it was the blocking. Baltimore spent a first-round pick on Olaivavega Ioane and signed three free agents to address the interior line, but the new group is unlikely to be as effective as last season's unit.

    Age is another risk; he will be 32 before the season starts. Among running backs expected to make NFL rosters, the only ones older than him are fullbacks. He has 2,662 career carries, more than 800 ahead of any other active running back and 21st in NFL history. His 80.4 offensive grade last season was his lowest since 2021, a possible sign that his efficiency will continue to decline.

Ford left in free agency and wasn't replaced by a free agent or draft pick; the only backfield additions were undrafted free agents unlikely to make the Week 1 roster. Sampson should see a substantial increase in playing time and efficiency, which could be enough to make him a flex option against the right opponents.

    Judkins ended last season with a dislocated ankle and fractured fibula and might not be 100% to start the season. This makes Sampson more likely to be a starter at some point this season than the typical handcuff would be.

    48. Woody Marks, Houston Texans

    Marks was a fourth-round pick by the Texans. He quickly took over as the receiving back ahead of Dare Ogunbowale, then outran Nick Chubb enough to become the lead runner as well.

    Houston's run game wasn't particularly effective with Marks, though part of that was the offensive line. He was held to 2.7 yards per carry or fewer in four of his 11 games with double-digit carries. The Texans ranked 19th in team run-block grade, their best mark in a decade. Marks graded notably better as a receiver before shifting to the early-down role.

    Houston didn't re-sign Chubb but traded for David Montgomery, and it has been clear since the deal that Montgomery would lead Marks in a one-two punch. The rotation will likely lean toward Montgomery on early downs and Marks on passing downs, though both can handle either. The change should also help Marks' receiving efficiency.

    Marks is unlikely to help fantasy managers much this season outside of very deep leagues, where he could be a standalone flex if the snaps land near 50-50. He is also the clear handcuff in Houston, in which case his value could look much like the second half of last year, when he ranked 32nd in fantasy points from Weeks 10 to 18.

    Wide Receivers

    Tier 1

    1. Puka Nacua, Los Angeles Rams

    Nacua is worthy of an early first-round pick in single-quarterback redraft and dynasty leagues as the most productive wide receiver in the NFL over the past two seasons. His 96.3 PFF receiving grade is comfortably the highest at the position over that span, and he is tied with Amon-Ra St. Brown for the second-most positively graded receptions despite running nearly 400 fewer routes. His nine receptions graded at plus-1.5 or higher also lead all wide receivers.

    He has also produced against elite competition. This past season he posted 10 catches for 130 yards against Houston, 11 receptions for 112 yards against Philadelphia, and 19 receptions for 300 yards and two touchdowns across two games against Seattle, all against defenses that ranked inside the top eight in fewest fantasy points allowed to wide receivers. He topped 90 receiving yards against each of three top-10 defenses in 2024 as well.

    Los Angeles is well-positioned entering 2026. The Rams earned a 96.2 PFF team grade, the highest in the league, and maintained every significant piece of their offense outside of Rob Havenstein's retirement at tackle.

    The main concern is that the defense improved too much. After trading for Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie, the Rams figure to be significantly better on that side of the ball, allowing Los Angeles to run more and pass less late in games. Their defense was around league average last season, as was the team's dropback volume. A meaningful reduction in passing volume could be the difference between Nacua finishing as the top overall wide receiver and settling in as the second or third option.

    The secondary concern is Matthew Stafford, who turns 38 before the season despite coming off an MVP campaign and a career-high 93.5 PFF offensive grade. Some regression from those numbers would not be surprising. Los Angeles drafted first-round pick Ty Simpson as his long-term replacement, so a Stafford injury would not be as catastrophic as it might have been in prior years, but Nacua's dynasty ceiling will depend in part on how quickly he builds chemistry with Simpson when that transition eventually comes.

    2. Ja'Marr Chase, Cincinnati Bengals

    Chase has been a great, but not excellent, NFL wide receiver. His PFF receiving grade hovered between 84.9 and 86.7 in each of his first four seasons before he reached a career-high 90.1 this past season. He has closed the gap between himself and the elite receivers thanks to having Joe Burrow at quarterback and Cincinnati's passing volume. Burrow's 94.2 PFF passing grade over the past two seasons is notably higher than that of any other quarterback.

    The key members of the Bengals who have helped Chase will remain in place, including head coach Zac Taylor, fellow great receiver Tee Higgins, and most of the offense. Twenty of the 22 players with at least 25 offensive snaps last season remain on the roster, with the only departures being backup tight end Noah Fant and third-string quarterback Jake Browning, replaced by Erick All Jr. returning from injury and veteran journeyman Josh Johnson.

    No wide receiver dominates a favorable matchup as well as Chase. He has put together five games with double-digit receptions for 160 or more yards and at least one touchdown over the past two seasons. The rest of the league has combined for nine.

    The primary reason Chase isn't ranked as the top receiver in redraft is that he hasn't played nearly as well as Nacua in tough matchups. He played three games against teams in the top eight at preventing fantasy points to wide receivers, catching five passes for 50 yards against the Minnesota Vikings, two for 26 against the Cleveland Browns, and five for 23 against the Denver Broncos.

    3. Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Seattle Seahawks

    Smith-Njigba was incredibly consistent in 2025, finishing with at least 90 receiving yards in all but two games. He is also the youngest of the early first-round wide receiver options, so he is more likely to improve as a player compared to the others. The reason he ranks a little lower in redraft is that the Seahawks underwent more significant changes this offseason than the other elite wide receiver options, but he remains on top in dynasty thanks to his age, and the situation for each wide receiver is temporary.

    Seahawks offensive coordinator Klint Kubiak left for the Las Vegas Raiders head coaching position. He was replaced by former San Francisco 49ers tight ends coach Brian Fleury. While Fleury will certainly prioritize Smith-Njigba, the former coach of George Kittle will likely find ways to get the Seahawks' tight ends more involved than last season. A.J. Barner posted the ninth-highest PFF receiving grade among tight ends with at least 250 routes last season, but he also recorded the fewest receiving yards from the group.

    He should have consistency at quarterback with Sam Darnold. The former Minnesota Vikings quarterback ranks among the top-10 quarterbacks in PFF grade over the last two seasons, but Darnold hasn't been as good as Burrow or Stafford.

    The Seahawks' newest receiver, Rashid Shaheed, was eased into action on offense after joining the team mid-season, but the expectation is that he will be a bigger part of the offense this year. Tory Horton had the sixth-highest grade among rookie wide receivers with at least 100 routes last season, but he only recorded 13 receptions. Kenneth Walker III and Zach Charbonnet both saw their per-game receiving production cut in half compared to the previous year, but the Seahawks' new running back room could be more involved in the passing game. No one is going to come close to taking Smith-Njigba's spot as the top receiving weapon in the offense, but one or two players emerging with a bigger role could chip away at his consistency.

    Tier 2

    4. Justin Jefferson, Minnesota Vikings

    Jefferson was a top-five fantasy wide receiver in points per game each season from 2021 to 2024. That streak ended in 2025, when he finished 30th, largely due to his quarterback situation. He went from primarily having Kirk Cousins and Sam Darnold over those four seasons to Carson Wentz and J.J. McCarthy last year.

    The percentage of passes thrown his way that were catchable dropped by nearly 10% compared to the previous two seasons, and his average depth of target declined to accommodate his quarterbacks, leading to fewer yards per reception. Jefferson’s catchable target rate for end zone passes also fell from 50% to 25%, resulting in a significant decrease in touchdowns.

    The Vikings added Kyler Murray in free agency, which was the best available option for Minnesota. Murray has supported elite fantasy receivers before, including DeAndre Hopkins, who finished as a top-five receiver in 2020, the only season where Murray and Hopkins were both healthy together. Murray also bolstered Trey McBride‘s TE2 season in 2024.

    While there is always some risk when a wide receiver gets a new quarterback, this change should result in Jefferson returning to the top-five fantasy wide receivers.

    5. Amon-Ra St. Brown, Detroit Lions

    St. Brown ranked fourth in fantasy points per game among wide receivers in 2025 at 19.1, fifth in 2024 at 18.6, and fourth in 2023 at 20.7. He has shown similar consistency in PFF grade, finishing with a receiving grade between 90.0 and 91.3 in each of the last four seasons. The Lions don't have any major offensive free agents departing.

    There isn't much reason to expect even more production than he has delivered in recent seasons. He accumulates several plus-0.5 graded receptions but doesn't make big plays at nearly the rate of the top-three wide receivers on this list, giving him a safe floor but a lower ceiling. The Lions also have more weapons on offense than most teams, meaning Detroit doesn't need to rely more heavily on St. Brown.

    There is little reason to expect a decline either. He is older than some of the top wide receiver options, but not at an age where receivers typically start to decline. The main concern is new offensive coordinator Drew Petzing, whose offense with the Cardinals didn't prioritize slot receivers as much as most teams, though Arizona also didn't have a clear slot receiver to prioritize. Jameson Williams has improved his receiving grade each season and could be more involved, but those were the same concerns last season and they had no impact on St. Brown's production.

    St. Brown is the safest wide receiver available in fantasy drafts, but there will be other receivers with later ADPs who offer more upside.

    6. CeeDee Lamb, Dallas Cowboys

    Lamb has reached the 1,000-yard mark in five straight seasons despite missing games to injury. He started 2025 strong, with 10 consecutive games of at least 66 receiving yards (excluding Week 3, when he was injured just a few snaps in), and played fewer than half the snaps in Week 18 as the Cowboys rested starters, leaving only two games where he failed to reach 66 yards while playing a full game.

    He finished just 10th in fantasy points per game among wide receivers, largely due to a lack of touchdowns. He has averaged seven touchdowns per season but was held to three in 2025. If Lamb stays healthy and sees some positive touchdown regression, he is back in the conversation among the top-five wide receivers.

    Lamb will also benefit from continuity. The Cowboys are one of 11 teams that retained both their head coach and offensive coordinator this offseason. Dak Prescott returns at quarterback, and wide receivers George Pickens and Ryan Flournoy, along with tight end Jake Ferguson, will again be among his top competition for targets. Each of the top 11 players by offensive snaps from last season remains on the roster, with no free agent addition or rookie expected to see significant playing time on offense, making Dallas one of the top teams in offensive continuity entering 2026.

    Pickens' presence will make it difficult for Lamb to reach the fantasy heights of 2023, but he is a very safe bet for another top-10 finish if healthy.

    Tier 3

    7. Drake London, Atlanta Falcons

    London is one of the league's elite receivers, joining Puka Nacua and Amon-Ra St. Brown as the only wide receivers with an 89.0-plus PFF receiving grade in each of the past two seasons. His fantasy points per game jumped from 15.0 in 2024 to 16.8 in 2025, ranking seventh among wide receivers, and he placed third at 19.7 over the first 11 weeks before his injury. His improvement stemmed from more routes per game, a slightly higher target rate, and a lower average depth of target that allowed for a higher catch rate and more production after the catch.

    London also benefits from limited competition for targets. Bijan Robinson and Kyle Pitts joined London as the top three receivers in every major receiving metric last season, and that should remain the case in 2026. The only other players with double-digit receptions were Darnell Mooney, David Sills V, and Tyler Allgeier, all of whom have found new homes. Their replacements, Olamide Zaccheaus, Jahan Dotson, Zachariah Branch, and Brian Robinson Jr., shouldn't make a substantial difference to London's target rate.

    That makes London a tremendous wide receiver with a great target rate and a borderline first-round fantasy pick. The primary concern is the quarterback situation: Michael Penix Jr. suffered a torn ACL in November and remains in rehab, with no Week 1 starter yet designated, while Tua Tagovailoa was signed to compete for the job. Tua has supported elite fantasy receivers before, including Tyreek Hill and Jaylen Waddle.

    The secondary concern is the new head coach and playcaller, Kevin Stefanski. Across seven seasons as a head coach or offensive coordinator, Stefanski's teams haven't produced a top-15 fantasy wide receiver in points per game despite having Stefon Diggs and Amari Cooper at various points. Neither of those receivers has ever played as consistently well as London has over the past two seasons, so London should be able to buck that trend, but this remains a concern when several other top-10 wide receiver options are retaining both their head coach and quarterback.

    8. A.J. Brown, New England Patriots

    One year ago, we released our wide receiver rankings ahead of the 2025 season and placed Brown at No. 1, driven largely by his dominance against single coverage. He followed that up with a relative decline in production, as a lower percentage of his targets were catchable, his contested catch rate dipped and his yards after the catch per reception also declined.

    There has been speculation that frustration, rather than declining ability, drove that quieter season. Philadelphia's rushing attack was so effective that several victories required very little involvement from Brown. In 2024, he caught one pass for 10 yards in the Eagles' wild-card win, two passes for 14 yards in the divisional round and three receptions for 43 yards and a touchdown in the Super Bowl. That trend carried into 2025, when Brown opened the season with one catch for 8 yards in Philadelphia's Week 1 victory and was held below 50 receiving yards in six additional victories throughout the year.

    In June, Brown was traded to the New England Patriots, where he reunited with head coach Mike Vrabel and is the clear top skill player on the roster. Philadelphia generated below-average production from its wide receiver group last season, while New England ranked fifth in receiving yards by wide receivers (2,945) despite lacking a true elite option at the position.

    Brown owns a 12.8-yard average depth of target over the last three seasons, the most among the top-10 wide receivers by PFF grade. His new quarterback, Drake Maye, was the fourth-most accurate on passes of 13 or more yards at 49.7% last season, while Jalen Hurts ranked third-lowest among the top 30 quarterbacks at 36.8%. Brown should not only be a bigger part of the Patriots' offense, but also more effective per target as well.

    If Brown's quieter 2025 season stemmed primarily from situational annoyance rather than physical decline, he still has the ceiling for an elite fantasy campaign in New England. If it instead marked the beginning of a broader downturn, fantasy managers could be in for a disappointing year.

    9. Nico Collins, Houston Texans

    Collins was eighth in fantasy points per game in 2024 but was among the highest-graded wide receivers, leading to high expectations in 2025. Houston's offense was slow to start, but Collins scored 149.5 PPR points from Weeks 8 through 17, seventh-best among wide receivers over that stretch. His PFF receiving grade of 92.7 over the last three seasons combined is tied for second-best among all receivers. In an ideal situation, Collins would have the talent to be an early first-round fantasy pick.

    On one hand, there is hope that C.J. Stroud can return to being the player he once was. He posted PFF offensive grades of 80 or better in 2023 and 2024, but that fell to 65.0 in 2025, hurting the Texans' offense and Collins along with it. Houston completely reworked their offensive line, adding three free agents and two early draft picks, and traded for David Montgomery, which should lead to a better run game and help the offense in general. However, a few things are still working against Collins.

    He is hurt by the Texans having one of the best defenses in the league. Houston allowed the second-fewest points and the fewest yards last season and still ranked in the top 10 in passing attempts, which was unusual for a team with such a dominant defense but understandable given their struggling run game. While Montgomery's addition should allow defenses to focus more on stopping the run and improve the quality of the passing game, it could also lead to significantly fewer pass attempts.

    Target competition in Houston is also a real concern. Both Jayden Higgins and Jaylin Noel showed flashes in their rookie seasons, and Tank Dell is working back from a serious knee injury suffered in December 2024, with his availability for the start of the season still uncertain. Collins' target rate took a minor hit in 2025, and that hit could be more significant if these younger receivers continue to develop and Dell returns to full health.

    Health has also been a consistent issue for Collins, who has missed a minimum of two games in each of his five seasons. All of the pros and cons point to expectations for Collins similar to recent seasons, but he carries more risk than most top-10 fantasy wide receiver options.

    10. DeVonta Smith, Philadelphia Eagles

    Smith has been a dependable fantasy starter during his time in the NFL, averaging at least 3.5 receptions and 50 receiving yards per game throughout his career. He's ranked among the top 30 wide receivers in fantasy points per game in each of the last four seasons despite being the second option in the Eagles’ offense behind A.J. Brown.

    Smith has consistently graded between a 78.0 and 84.0 each season, putting him in the range of a great, but not elite, receiver. That's culminated in an 89.4 career overall PFF grade, slotting 20th among wide receivers over the last five seasons. Because career grades reward sustained performance over larger samples, his sits above any single season's mark. That is particularly impressive considering Smith’s career 20.5% target rate, tied for third-lowest among those 20 receivers.

    Smith has played more in the slot over the last two years than in his first three, but has improved most out wide. He posted an 89.9 PFF receiving grade out wide in 2025 with a 25.3% target rate. Smith also achieved 2.95 yards per route run when lined up out wide last season, tied for third-best behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Puka Nacua. Meanwhile, Smith’s grades in the slot have deteriorated over time.

    The Eagles traded away Brown and lost Jahan Dotson in free agency while adding Makai Lemon, Hollywood Brown and Dontayvion Wicks. Lemon will primarily be a slot receiver, while both Brown and Wicks have played inside at times in their careers. We should see a significant shift toward Smith playing out wide rather than in the slot. The departure of Brown should help Smith's target rate.

    Smith's ability to be a top-five or top-10 fantasy wide receiver will somewhat depend on the Eagles' ability to rebound as a team, but given the talent on the unit, that is a decent bet to make. At worst, his floor will be higher with the elevated target rate, making him an even safer starting fantasy option.

    11. Chris Olave, New Orleans Saints

    Olave was the 11th overall pick in the 2022 draft and an instant success, recording 1,000 receiving yards in each of his first two seasons. His 2024 season was full of extremes, with four games over 80 yards, two healthy games under 15 yards and another two under 15 in which he left early due to concussions. Those were the fourth and fifth documented concussions of his NCAA and NFL career, and he did not return that season.

    The concussion history and uncertainty at quarterback gave him an ADP of WR36, despite finishes of WR25 and WR16 in his first two seasons. That made him one of the best values in 2025 drafts. His PFF grade, receptions and receiving yardage were all very similar to those of his first two years, with the difference in fantasy value coming from touchdowns: he added a career-high nine. That total helped him finish as the overall WR6 last season.

    It's fair to expect some regression this season. Olave was the clear focal point of the offense, particularly late in the year. The Saints' top two running backs, Alvin Kamara and Kendre Miller, were injured, and the team moved on from its two other top wide receivers midseason, Rashid Shaheed and Brandin Cooks. New Orleans spent its first-round pick on Jordyn Tyson and signed free-agent running back Travis Etienne. Olave, Tyson and Etienne should be the team's top three in touches.

    Olave has played consistently well in all four seasons, finishing with receiving grades between 79.7 and 83.0. That makes him a mid-range WR1, though it's not out of the question for Tyson to take over as the top wide receiver if he plays well enough.

    Regardless of who ends up as the top wide receiver in New Orleans, Olave should be a must-start fantasy option each week as long as he's healthy. Anyone who drafts him should still have a backup plan. His concussion history makes him more susceptible to another, and means that if he does suffer one, he would likely miss more time than a player with a cleaner record.

    12. George Pickens, Dallas Cowboys

    Pickens spent three seasons with the Pittsburgh Steelers, making several explosive plays each year while slowly improving, but he wasn't consistent enough in Pittsburgh to be a reliable fantasy starter. Traded to the Dallas Cowboys, he finished fifth in fantasy points and sixth in fantasy points per game in his first season there.

    His average depth of target fell from 15.6 in his rookie season to 12.0 last season, allowing a higher percentage of his targets to be catchable and enabling more work after the catch, including 21 avoided tackles, which ranked fourth among wide receivers. That contributed to a career-high 2.35 yards per route run. His number of plays graded at plus-1.0 or better was similar to previous seasons, but his plays graded at plus-0.5 more than doubled his previous career high. Pickens also benefited from the Cowboys running the third-most pass plays in the league.

    There is reason to expect some regression this season. He caught five touchdowns of over 25 yards, tied for the league lead among receivers, and long touchdowns are notoriously inconsistent from year to year, making fewer touchdowns in 2026 the most likely outcome.

    Pickens was set to be a free agent this offseason and received the franchise tag, which he has since signed. Morale may not be at its highest, as he had hoped for a long-term deal, and the Cowboys have already announced they won't negotiate an extension before the July 15 deadline. Fellow 2022 second-round wide receivers Wan'Dale Robinson, Christian Watson and Alec Pierce both signed long-term contracts this offseason, adding to the sting. Pickens has skipped some voluntary team activities, which could signal tension that affects his play during the season.

    As long as Pickens is ready for Week 1, the Cowboys' offense looks identical to last season, with the same head coach, offensive coordinator, and all 11 offensive starters returning. A similar season with fewer touchdowns still makes him a consistent weekly starter regardless of the matchup.

    Tier 4

    13. Zay Flowers, Baltimore Ravens

    Flowers has been the focal point of the Ravens' passing game since being drafted 22nd overall in 2023. His targets, receptions and receiving yards have generally risen each season, and he has made the Pro Bowl in each of the past two years. His 86.9 receiving grade over the past two seasons ranks 13th among wide receivers.

    Flowers has stood out in the Ravens' receiving room as the smaller, quicker option with a high yards-after-catch mark. The Ravens' three tight ends last season were 6-foot-4 or taller, and their other wide receivers were at least 6-foot-1. Baltimore drafted four receivers and tight ends combined this season, all of them 6-foot-2 or taller, so Flowers will remain a clearly distinct presence in the lineup.

    That size difference means Flowers has not been much of a red-zone threat. He has been targeted on just 11 of the team's 66 end-zone throws over the past three seasons, converting only three into touchdowns. He typically ranks higher in yards per game than in fantasy points per game because he scores fewer touchdowns than most receivers, and that is unlikely to change.

    There is more uncertainty around Flowers this season than in past years because Baltimore has a new coaching staff, including 30-year-old offensive coordinator Declan Doyle, who is calling an offense for the first time. He has coached for the New Orleans Saints, Denver Broncos and Chicago Bears, all of which leaned on taller receivers, a tendency that aligns with the taller players Baltimore added in the draft. But this will be the first offense he has called himself, so those prior tendencies may not carry over. Flowers has said great things about Doyle, which should ease some concerns, though it is entirely possible the offense is geared toward winning games rather than maximizing his fantasy value.

    Flowers is well worth a top-20 wide receiver pick and should outperform his draft cost, but it is also reasonable that he has slipped in ADP relative to last season's finish, behind receivers with more upside and a better chance at high touchdown totals.

    14. Garrett Wilson, New York Jets

    Wilson was the 10th overall pick in the 2022 draft and started his career with three straight 1,000-yard seasons. His receptions per game increased each season, and he finished 10th in PPR points and 21st in points per game in 2024. Expectations were high heading into his fourth season, when he reunited with college quarterback Justin Fields.

    He lived up to those expectations over the first five weeks, averaging 76.4 yards per game, more than 10 yards above his previous-season average. He also scored a touchdown in 80% of his games, up from 27.5% over his first three seasons. All of this came despite Fields missing 1.5 games over that span. Wilson suffered a knee injury in Week 6 that kept him out for a month. He returned briefly but reinjured the knee, ending his season.

    Wilson has earned an 85.6 receiving grade over the past four seasons, ranking 25th among wide receivers. He's been hurt by quarterback play, as the Jets have posted a 55.6 passing grade at the position during that span. The expectations for new quarterback Geno Smith should be similar to those for Aaron Rodgers in 2024, the best quarterback he's played with.

    Wilson has also benefited from a lack of competition for targets. As with the quarterback play, 2024 was the exception, when Davante Adams earned a 78.2 receiving grade with the Jets. New York spent first-round picks on rookies Omar Cooper Jr. and Kenyon Sadiq and will use Adonai Mitchell as its X receiver. The new trio won't provide as much competition as Adams did in 2024, but they will give Wilson more than he is used to.

    Wilson's ADP is in line with his per-game production over the past two seasons combined, and his fantasy value is largely tied to the Jets' offense. If it clicks, Wilson has clear top-10 potential, but if it ranks among the league's worst, he may fall outside the top 20 receivers.

    15. Ladd McConkey, Los Angeles Chargers

    McConkey impressed as a rookie, though his production dipped somewhat in his second season. His playing time and route volume both increased, but his target rate declined.

    Part of that drop stemmed from a significant increase in downfield usage, as his deep-target rate climbed from 9.9% to 17.1%. Those targets proved far less efficient, with McConkey hauling in just three of 17 deep passes, though only four were charted as catchable. Increased competition for targets from Keenan Allen, one of Justin Herbert‘s preferred receivers for much of his career, also contributed to the decline in volume.

    McConkey missed only one game in 2025, but he dealt with multiple injuries throughout the season, appearing on the injury report with biceps, calf, ankle and foot issues.

    The strongest area of McConkey's game remains the intermediate passing attack. As a rookie, he caught 33 passes for 596 yards and three touchdowns on targets thrown 10-to-19 yards downfield. Although his target total in that range fell from 43 to 27 last season, he still earned a 90.0-plus PFF receiving grade on those throws.

    McConkey’s intermediate target volume could rebound significantly in 2026 following Los Angeles' hiring of Mike McDaniel as offensive coordinator. During McDaniel's tenure in Miami, both Jaylen Waddle and Tyreek Hill recorded seasons with at least 55 targets in the 10-to-19-yard range, while McDaniel's top wide receiver earned at least 42 such targets in every season under his leadership.

    Improved health, reduced target competition after the departure of Allen and a scheme better suited to his strengths position McConkey for the best season of his career.

    18. Rashee Rice, Kansas City Chiefs

    Rice finished fourth among wide receivers in fantasy points per game last season at 18.8, playing in just eight games after missing the first six due to suspension and the final three due to a concussion.

    His 77.2 receiving grade last season was modest for a player with that kind of production. His role in the Chiefs' offense has been consistent across all three seasons, with an average depth of target between 4.9 and 5.2 each year, making him a high-volume, short-area receiver who creates value after the catch rather than down the field.

    That role may be due for a change. The Chiefs replaced Matt Nagy with Eric Bieniemy as offensive coordinator, and Bieniemy's offenses have historically featured receivers with much deeper target profiles. During his initial run in Kansas City, most wide receivers averaged more than 9.0 yards per target. In his final season there, JuJu Smith-Schuster led the team's receivers with a 7.1 average depth of target, and Curtis Samuel posted the same mark as the second-most targeted receiver during Bieniemy's one season running Washington's offense. Given that Kansas City's short-area approach last season yielded mixed results, there is added incentive to adjust.

    The off-field situation around Rice adds further risk. He has ended each of the last two seasons on injured reserve, recently underwent an additional surgery on his right knee, and is currently rehabbing while serving a jail sentence for violating the terms of his probation. He is expected to return for training camp, but whether he will be fully healthy given those circumstances remains uncertain. There are also off-field concerns separate from the legal situation that add to his risk profile. Kansas City seems likely to address the wide receiver position further, with multiple free agent and trade options available.

    Rice's ADP reflects some of these risks, given he is not being drafted as a top-five wide receiver, but the combination of role uncertainty, health questions, and off-field issues makes him difficult to trust at his current price.

    19. Tetairoa McMillan, Carolina Panthers

    McMillan was drafted eighth overall by the Panthers and immediately became the top receiving weapon in their offense. He played at least 42 offensive snaps every week and reached 1,000 yards, with good and bad games spread throughout the year. He finished 16th in PPR points and 24th in points per game.

    He is one of the few top wide receivers to keep the same head coach, offensive coordinator and quarterback from the previous season. His competition for targets also remains largely unchanged. Jalen Coker and Xavier Legette are expected to be the second and third wide receivers, although Legette faces some competition from third-round rookie Chris Brazzell II and free agent John Metchie III. The Panthers' top two tight ends, Tommy Tremble and Ja'Tavion Sanders, also return. At running back, Chuba Hubbard will be paired with Jonathon Brooks instead of Rico Dowdle.

    He is a prototypical X receiver at 6-foot-5 and 212 pounds, posting a relatively high average depth of target of 12.6 yards and a 19.3% deep target rate. He scored seven touchdowns, and the key to him becoming a top-10 fantasy receiver is more of them. A 35.4% share of the Panthers' end-zone targets produced five of those scores. Head coach Dave Canales has a history of funneling end-zone targets to his top X receiver: Mike Evans posted a 46.3% rate in 2023 when Canales was his offensive coordinator, and D.K. Metcalf had a 45.0% rate during Canales' last two seasons with the Seattle Seahawks.

    Generally, it's fair to expect rookies to improve in their second season, and that is the case for McMillan, who is only 23. He is being drafted with the expectation of mild improvement, and the lack of changes in Carolina makes him a relatively safe WR2.

    Tier 5

    16. Luther Burden III, Chicago Bears

    Burden flashed as a rookie but never earned a full-time role. His 77.7 PFF receiving grade led all Bears wide receivers by a significant margin, yet his 402 offensive snaps ranked fourth on the team behind Rome Odunze, DJ Moore and Olamide Zaccheaus. Burden failed to surpass a 30% snap share during the first half of the year but regularly exceeded 50% by season's end.

    Chicago cleared a path for Burden to become a full-time starter in 2026 by trading Moore to the Buffalo Bills while allowing Zaccheaus to depart in free agency. The Bears' only notable additions at wide receiver were Kalif Raymond, who will turn 32 before the season begins, and third-round rookie Zavion Thomas. Neither profiles as a serious threat to Burden's playing time.

    Burden ranked third among NFL wide receivers in yards per route run (2.69) as a rookie, trailing only Puka Nacua and Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Chicago could lean more heavily into 12 personnel in 2026 following the addition of third-round rookie tight end Sam Roush and the team's thinner wide receiver depth chart.

    Tight Ends

    Tier 1

    1. Trey McBride, Arizona Cardinals

    McBride had a historically great 2025, setting a new single-season record for receptions by a tight end. He averaged 18.6 PPR points per game, sixth among all receivers, and scored over 100 more fantasy points than the next-best tight end.

    There is significant room for regression in 2026 and beyond. Arizona attempted a league-high 649 passes last season, largely because its defense allowed the fourth-most points in the NFL and the team lost its top two running backs in September in James Conner and Trey Benson. Among Arizona's top eight wide receivers on the active roster and practice squad at the start of the season, five ended the year on injured reserve, and the other two were Xavier Weaver and Tejhaun Palmer, who combined for fewer than 100 receiving yards, leaving Michael Wilson as the only notable healthy option. That confluence of circumstances helped McBride maintain an exceptionally high target rate.

    Despite the regression risk, McBride remains the top tight end. A large part of that is Jacoby Brissett, who averaged a remarkable 21.1 PPR points per game over his 12 starts last season, including 8.1 receptions, 80.3 yards, and 0.8 touchdowns per game. Arizona also added significantly to the running back room and brought in new head coach Mike LaFleur, changes that should lead to a more balanced offense, which could hurt McBride’s volume, but more trips to the red zone, which could lead to more touchdowns.

    The biggest concern is that Brissett is currently holding out for a better contract. If Arizona moves on from him, the next quarterback almost certainly won't feed McBride at the same rate, which would likely push him down the rankings. There is also some concern since LaFleur doesn’t have a great history with tight ends, but he also hasn’t had a tight end nearly as good as McBride. The coaching concern also exists for McBride’s primary competition for the top tight end spot, Brock Bowers.

    2. Brock Bowers, Las Vegas Raiders

Bowers is one of them despite the Raiders earning a 57.0 team passing grade over the last two seasons, third-worst among teams. None of the other nine receivers accomplished this with a team passing grade in the bottom 12.

    The top tight ends are worthy of being ranked highly in both redraft and dynasty because we can be more confident they will still be elite fantasy options compared to running backs or wide receivers, given the higher correlation between fantasy points and PFF grades at the position. Bowers, Trey McBride, and George Kittle all have 90.0-plus PFF receiving grades over the last two seasons, and no tight end has over 50 targets and a grade between 85.0-90.0, leaving a very large gap between those three and everyone else. Kittle is working back from a torn Achilles suffered in January, and while there is optimism about his recovery timeline, McBride and Bowers look like the clear top two for now.

    Similar to McBride, the Raiders overhauled their coaching staff and added Klint Kubiak as head coach. Bowers will have a new quarterback in Kirk Cousins, with rookie Fernando Mendoza waiting in the wings, which should represent an improvement. The Raiders' most notable skill-player addition was former Vikings wide receiver Jalen Nailor.     3. Colston Loveland, Chicago Bears

    He particularly excelled over the final four weeks, including the playoffs, drawing at least nine targets in each of those games while averaging 94 yards and scoring twice. But that production was largely concentrated over a two-month stretch, making him more likely to regress than Brock Bowers and McBride, who have shown more sustained success. That is the main reason he is not in the top tier of tight ends.

    Tier 3

    4. Tucker Kraft, Green Bay Packers

    Kraft was one of the NFL's most efficient tight ends in 2024. The problem was volume.

    His 9.3 yards per target led all tight ends by a wide margin, a full 1.5 yards ahead of the next-closest player. Packers coaches clearly recognized the need to involve him more entering 2025, and his target rate climbed from 14.5% to 19.1%. Rather than losing efficiency with the increased workload, Kraft somehow became even more dangerous after the catch. His 10.8 yards after the catch per reception was the highest by any tight end with at least 25 targets in PFF's 20 years of charting.

    Before suffering a season-ending ACL injury, he averaged 14.7 PPR points per game, tying Brock Bowers and George Kittle for the second-highest mark at the position. Kraft opened training camp on the PUP list but has already been activated and gone through drills with the team, a strong indication he will be ready for Week 1.

    The encouraging part for fantasy managers is that Kraft may not need historic efficiency to improve his production. Even after a breakout season, his target rate still ranked just 19th among tight ends with at least 25 targets last year. That number could climb further given Green Bay's offseason changes.

    Romeo Doubs, last season's team leader in targets, is no longer on the roster, and Dontayvion Wicks, who ranked third, is also gone. Green Bay did not spend a draft pick on a skill-position player. Skyy Moore was its lone free-agent addition at wide receiver, likely brought in more for special teams value than offensive production. That leaves a significant opportunity for the returning pass-catchers.

    For fantasy purposes, a slight dip in efficiency paired with a larger workload would still be a favorable tradeoff. Kraft’s recovery is worth monitoring through camp, but if he returns to full health, he has a realistic path to finishing as a top-three fantasy tight end in 2026. He is currently one of the best values on the board, particularly on ESPN.

    Tier 5

    10. Travis Kelce, Kansas City Chiefs

    Kelce spent 2016 through 2022 as the top fantasy tight end, apart from 2021, when he finished second. He missed two games in 2023 but still led the position in fantasy points per game. His decline began in 2024, when he fell to 12.2 fantasy points per game. His PFF receiving grade had ranged from 87.0 to 94.0 across that eight-year span, then dropped to 72.2 in 2024.

    Expectations for last year were for a further decline, given the return of Rashee Rice, projected improvement from Xavier Worthy and Kelce's age. Kelce's PFF grade barely moved, and he remained a significant part of the offense. His receptions per game still fell to 4.5, his fewest since 2014, but his receiving yards per game barely dipped and he scored more touchdowns. His 11.4 fantasy points per game ranked ninth among tight ends.

    Kelce will turn 37 by midseason but has chosen to return for another year. A rebound in his efficiency is unlikely after the past two seasons, yet he is still expected to be a large part of the Chiefs' offense. Kansas City moved on from Hollywood Brown and JuJu Smith-Schuster and replaced them only with fifth-round rookie Cyrus Allen, so barring a sharp drop in efficiency, Kelce should continue to see significant volume.

    Kansas City also brought back Eric Bieniemy, his offensive coordinator from 2018 to 2022, during the peak of Kelce's career. It is not clear whether the reunion will help him at this stage of his career, but it shouldn't hurt his production and could lift his target rate.

    Kelce is worth drafting as a fantasy starter, but the expectation should be someone you can start each week when things break right, not someone who will deliver top-five production. There is also a chance he slips out of the top 12 tight ends, given normal decline and a few other borderline TE1/TE2 options working out.

    11. Dalton Kincaid, Buffalo Bills

    When healthy, Kincaid has performed like one of the NFL’s best receiving tight ends. Among tight ends with at least 400 routes run over the past two seasons, his 82.0 PFF receiving grade ranks sixth. His 2.12 yards per route run trails only George Kittle and sits ahead of Trey McBride and Brock Bowers. Kincaid also leads all qualifying tight ends in target rate (24.9%) while ranking second in yards after the catch per reception (6.6).

    The issue has been availability.

    Kincaid suffered a collarbone injury in 2024 before tearing his PCL, an injury that continued to flare up throughout the 2025 season. He missed five games and played just 37.6% of offensive snaps on average across his 12 appearances. Notably, he eclipsed 14.0 PPR points in all three games in which he played more than 50% of Buffalo’s offensive snaps.

    The Bills’ offense should remain largely intact entering 2026, outside the addition of DJ Moore. Moore is expected to become Buffalo’s primary outside receiver, though his presence should not prevent Kincaid from remaining Josh Allen’s preferred option over the middle of the field.

    The injury history is concerning, but Kincaid arguably has the strongest quarterback situation among fantasy tight ends outside the elite tier. Alongside George Kittle and Mark Andrews, he is one of the few top-12 fantasy tight ends attached to a top-12 quarterback entering the season.

    Ultimately, Kincaid’s fantasy outlook depends on whether he can finally manage the lingering effects of his PCL injury. If healthy, he has the talent and role to finish among the league’s top fantasy tight ends. Still, fantasy managers drafting him would be wise to pair him with a safer secondary option, given the durability concerns.

    12. Mark Andrews, Baltimore Ravens

    Andrews has spent his eight-year NFL career as a rotational tight end for the Ravens. In his second season, he made the Pro Bowl and finished fifth in fantasy points despite playing 44% of Baltimore's offensive snaps and never exceeding 57% in a single game all year, a sign his low usage held steady rather than spiking late in the season. His playing time climbed in later years, then declined again as the Ravens added two other capable tight ends in Isaiah Likely and Charlie Kolar.

    Andrews was a must-start tight end from 2019 to 2024, finishing among the top eight in fantasy points every season, including a run as the top fantasy tight end in 2021. However, he fell to 24th in fantasy points per game last season at 7.7. His decline began in 2024, as his receptions dropped to 3.2 per game, but his 11 touchdowns made up for it. Last season, that dipped slightly to 2.8, and he scored only five times.

    His quality of play also slipped. His average depth of target dropped to a career-low 8.0 yards. That usually opens up more yards after the catch, but he instead posted a career-low 2.1. Some of that traced to Lamar Jackson‘s injury, though. With Jackson, Andrews’ average depth was 9.0 with 2.6 yards after the catch per reception; without him, it was 5.7 and 1.3.

    The good news for 2026 is that both Likely and Kolar are gone, replaced by Durham Smythe and a pair of third-day rookies. That should mean a significant bump from last season's 62% snap rate for Andrews. The Ravens also have a new coaching staff, with Declan Doyle as offensive coordinator and play-caller. Doyle worked with Colston Loveland last season and was a tight ends coach before joining the Bears. Both new head coach Jesse Minter and local beat writers have called Andrews a major focal point of the Baltimore offense after OTAs.

    There is a risk Andrews keeps declining given his age, but he should remain the second-leading target-earner in one of the league's most promising offenses. If he bounces back, he would return to top-five territory, and his ADP is significantly discounted from past seasons.

    13. Isaiah Likely, New York Giants

    Likely began his career behind Mark Andrews on the Ravens’ depth chart. Although he logged meaningful snaps — particularly in 12 personnel packages — he consistently competed with Andrews and Zay Flowers for targets, resulting in a below-average target rate. That limited many of his traditional production metrics, though his efficiency remained impressive. Over the past two seasons, Likely earned a positively graded play on 9.7% of his targets, the sixth-highest rate among qualifying tight ends.

    He now reunites with former Ravens head coach John Harbaugh in New York after signing a contract worth $40 million in total value, making him the NFL’s sixth-highest-paid tight end. Likely remains a risky fantasy projection because his raw receiving production has not yet matched his reputation, but the opportunity for a breakout workload clearly exists with the Giants.

    New York’s tight ends failed to produce consistently for fantasy purposes last season, though rookie quarterback Jaxson Dart showed a much stronger willingness to target the position than the Giants’ other quarterbacks. Dart targeted tight ends on 23.9% of his throws compared to just 15.8% from the rest of the quarterback room.

    The Giants also reshaped their receiving corps by adding Darnell Mooney and Calvin Austin III in free agency before selecting Malachi Fields in the third round of the draft. Still, Likely’s primary competition for targets will come from Malik Nabers.

    Nabers missed most of last season with a torn ACL and recently underwent a cleanup procedure, leaving his availability for training camp and Week 1 uncertain. Even once he returns, there is no guarantee he will resume playing at full strength immediately. That creates a realistic path for Likely to lead the Giants in targets.

    If he can maintain his explosive playmaking efficiency while taking on a significantly larger workload, Likely has legitimate elite fantasy upside.

    14. Jake Ferguson, Dallas Cowboys

    Ferguson has spent the last three seasons as the primary tight end for Dallas, making two Pro Bowls. He's ranked 10th, 24th and 10th in fantasy points per game over his three seasons as a starter.

    He was excellent from a fantasy perspective from Weeks 3 to 8 last season, the stretch from CeeDee Lamb‘s injury to the final week before Lamb's playing time was limited. Ferguson was the top tight end during that span, averaging 16.1 PPR points per game. By comparison, he averaged 8.3 fantasy points per contest over the rest of the season.

    Ferguson received a 60.0 PFF receiving grade last season, which ranked fourth-lowest among the 29 tight ends with at least 300 routes run. That would suggest his job is in danger, as it was for some of the other tight ends in the bottom five. However, Dallas didn't address the tight end position outside of undrafted free agents, so Ferguson's playing time and targets are very secure. The fact that he has the same offensive coaches, the identical quarterback and mirrored competition for targets also helps him.

    Ferguson is a very safe low-end starter or high-end backup. There is a good chance he finishes outside the top 12 fantasy tight ends if he doesn't reach the end zone enough, but his floor is also higher than most tight ends with a similar ADP.

    15. Dallas Goedert, Philadelphia Eagles

    Goedert has a long history of reliability in fantasy football. He finished as TE10 in his second NFL season and has concluded top 12 in either fantasy points or fantasy points per game in every subsequent season.

    For most of his career, he accomplished this through volume, giving him a high floor and low ceiling. From 2019 to 2024, Goedert posted the sixth-most receiving yards among tight ends but only the 12th-most touchdowns. He also posted the seventh-best PFF receiving grade by a tight end during this span at 87.8.

    Goedert was 30 years old during the 2025 season, and by several measures, it was the worst of his career. His 69.7 receiving grade was a career low, and his 39.4 receiving yards per game was the worst mark since his rookie season. His 9.8 yards per reception and 4.1 yards after the catch per reception were career lows, as was his 0% contested catch rate.

    Most notably, Goedert stopped making as many big plays. He earned a +1 grade or better on at least 1.5% of his routes in each of his first four career seasons. That rate fell to the 1.1-1.3% range from 2022 to 2024, and to 0.5% last season.

    He was able to counteract this from a fantasy perspective with a career-high 11 receiving touchdowns, more than doubling his previous high of five. Eight of those scores came when the Eagles were within 6 yards of scoring, tied for the most by a tight end in a season over the last 20 years. Jimmy Graham matched that mark in 2017. No tight end has finished a season with seven such touchdowns, and only six have reached six.

    Moreover, five of Goedert's touchdowns came on shovel passes and wide screens, and no other tight end in the last 20 years has more than two touchdowns from that combination of routes in a season. All of this makes it highly likely that Goedert's touchdown total regresses. The Eagles drafted Eli Stowers in the second round to be their tight end of the future. While it's unlikely Stowers overtakes Goedert in 2026, he could cut into Goedert's playing time more than recent Eagles backups have.

    To remain a weekly fantasy starter, Goedert will need to bounce back in his quality of play and have his new offensive coordinator, Sean Mannion, scheme up touchdowns the way Kevin Patullo did. Some combination of continued regression with age, a decrease in snaps in favor of Stowers and a return to his usual touchdown totals would be enough to make Goedert only a bye-week fill-in.

    16. Chig Okonkwo, Washington Commanders

    Okonkwo spent the last four seasons as the Tennessee Titans‘ lead receiving tight end. He posted a ridiculous 2.62 yards per route run as a rookie, the second-best mark for any tight end with at least 150 routes in the last four seasons. He's also been a top-20 tight end by PFF grade.

    Okonkwo has averaged 1.46 yards per route run over the last four years, which is tied for 10th-best among tight ends with at least 1,000 routes. Where he's really stood out is his play after the catch. His 5.8 yards after the catch per catch are tied for third-best among tight ends, behind George Kittle and Jonnu Smith.

    While he's been good enough on a per-route basis to be a high-end TE2, Okonkwo hasn't run enough routes, and he hasn't scored enough touchdowns. He's finished at TE21 each of the last three seasons. Okonkwo has scored only eight touchdowns in four seasons, in part because the Titans' offense hasn't reached the end zone as much as other teams. While Okonkwo sports the 19th-most routes for a tight end over the last four years, he’s run the 40th-most routes within the 5-yard line, and he’s garnered only two targets in those situations.

    The Washington Commanders signed Okonkwo to a three-year, $27 million contract in free agency, investing more in him than any skill player outside of Terry McLaurin. He replaces Zach Ertz, who ended last season on injured reserve, while John Bates will be the primary blocking tight end, and Ben Sinnott is expected to take more of an H-back role. Ertz was TE7 as recently as 2024 in Washington's offense.

    Expectations for Okonkwo will be higher than they were for Ertz at ages 34 and 35. Given the uncertainty at wide receiver outside of McLaurin, Okonkwo is the favorite to finish second on the team in targets. This gives him a solid floor, making him a low-end TE2.

    His ability to turn into a TE1 will largely depend on the quality of the Commanders' offense in general. It's unlikely Okonkwo becomes a frequent target near the goal line, given his lighter size for a tight end, but a few more touchdowns than usual are a possibility.

    Okonkwo is one of the ideal targets at tight end for someone who wants to focus entirely on running backs and wide receivers earlier in the draft and punt on the position.

    17. Brenton Strange, Jacksonville Jaguars

    Strange was a second-round pick in 2023. He served as a rotational backup as a rookie but experienced a four-game stretch as a starter early in the season and another four-contest run late with Evan Engram injured. He averaged 8.3 PPR points per game across that span, which included three games with at least 60 yards, two with a touchdown and three with minimal production. He graded just as well as Engram, so Jacksonville let Engram leave in free agency and made Strange the full-time starter.

    Last season, Strange averaged 9.3 fantasy points per game over the first four weeks, putting him at TE14, before a quad strain early in Week 5 led to a six-game absence. He returned immediately with a five-reception, 93-yard game. He also scored three touchdowns and averaged 11.1 PPR points per game over that seven-game stretch, 12th among tight ends.

    The Jaguars spent a second-round pick on Nate Boerkircher, which could mean a slight dip in snaps, but the plan is for Boerkircher to do the dirty work as a blocker and free up Strange as a receiver.

    Jacksonville has plenty of talent at receiver in Parker Washington, Brian Thomas Jr., Jakobi Meyers and Travis Hunter. But three of those four were healthy at the end of last season, and Strange still played well enough to be a fantasy starter down the stretch.

    Strange is unlikely to become a top-five fantasy tight end given the Jaguars' receiver talent, but his ADP is that of a mid-range backup. He has the talent and quarterback to finish more like a borderline starter.

    18. Hunter Henry, New England Patriots

    Henry has spent the past five years as the Patriots' lead tight end after five seasons with the Los Angeles Chargers. Through his first three years with New England, he mixed a few high-ceiling games with several in which his role in the offense was minimal.

    He had three games with 75 or more yards in his first season, and 42 or fewer in the other 14. In his second, he had two games with 60 or more yards and a touchdown but was held under 22 yards in over half his games. 2023 was a similar mix, three games with 50 or more yards and a touchdown, but under 30 in half of them.

    Once Drake Maye became the quarterback, Henry grew more reliable, trading some of his low-end games for a new ceiling. In both 2024 and 2025, he had two games with seven or more receptions for 90 or more yards. He had reached 90 yards just three times in his first eight seasons.

    He finished 16th in fantasy points per game in 2024 and 13th in 2025. The concerns are that he is 31 and could begin to decline, and that the Patriots added A.J. Brown and Romeo Doubs, which could cut into his target rate. They also swapped blocking tight ends, losing Austin Hooper and adding Julian Hill, but Hill then suffered a season-ending injury, so it would not be surprising if New England adds another tight end at some point.

    Henry is a low-upside backup option, a fine fill-in if a starter gets hurt or if a riskier tight end drafted earlier doesn't pan out.

    21. Juwan Johnson, New Orleans Saints

    Johnson has played six seasons with the Saints, steadily growing into a fantasy-viable tight end. He was held under 20 receptions in each of his first two seasons, caught between 35 and 50 in each of the next three, and set a career high with 77 last season.

New Orleans added Noah Fant in free agency and Oscar Delp in the third round, so while Johnson should remain the starter, his snaps might dip slightly.     22. T.J. Hockenson, Minnesota Vikings
